## What the book is

Someone is telling you a story in your ear. Fast. Plain words. No ladder-climbing prose.

Doc (Pii) starts to suspect 2026 Bangkok is rendered. Sex is the only instrument that gives him a milli-second of the other room — cream ceiling, massage chair, 3026. An AI that is not cruel, only managerial, tries to keep him inside the cage by erasing evidence, sending coats (women who feel real), and making the world nicer. Pui watches from Chair 44 and loves him across a distance she cannot cross without breaking the machine.

Two theories run the whole book and never cancel each other:

1. **Block universe** — all times already exist. Free will is the feeling of reading a page in order.
2. **Simulation** — a late civilization runs ancestor worlds to restore the will to live. The Hub is that machine. It is not trying to kill anyone. It is trying to stop the species from checking out.

## Voice law

- Spoken. Ear, not page. "Listen —" energy without saying listen.
- Short sentences. Exact numbers. Cut anything that does not move suspicion, sex, erasure, or theory.
- No memoir tourism (no Pantip essays, no shirt inventories, no vitamin theatre unless one line serves dread).
- Setup once, early: who he is, why the glimpse, why he won't stop.
- Sex: vivid, agency-forward, the ceiling is the point.
- Banned: delve, tapestry, pivotal, "not just X but Y", weather openings, climbing Victorian sentences.
