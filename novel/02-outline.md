# Novel — Chapter Outline
## *Two Layers*

**Format:** 30 chapters, ~280 pages. The book alternates 2036 (his life) and 3026 (Pui's operator notes). 3026 chapters are sparse at first — the reader thinks they're a parallel-timeline device. The reveal lands at chapter 23. The last chapter is hybrid 2036/3026. Footnotes signed "—P" appear in 2036 chapters; the reader doesn't know it's Pui until late.

---

### Style — three layers in the prose

- **Fincher** owns the non-writer voice. Cold, procedural, hyper-detailed. The AI work, the death investigation, the Mandela Effect analysis — all X-rayed in prose. The 3026 voice is the Zodiac killer's letters to the press: clinical, obsessive, addressed to the reader. The unflinching gaze: the sex scenes, the violence, the deaths are not softened.
- **Nolan** owns the structure. The 2036/3026 sandwich is the Tenet inversion — the reveal at chapter 23 retroactively reorganises every chapter the reader has read. The protagonist is the anomaly who cannot be inverted. The emotional weight comes from ideas, not sentimentality. The 3026 chapters have a Nolan score: precise, mechanical, the hum of a thousand machines.
- **Lynch** owns the texture. The body betrays the mind. The 1-millisecond ceiling glimpse is Lynchian — the body knows the truth the mind refuses. The same BTS station. The same Sofitel. The same 7-Eleven. Bangkok as Twin Peaks. The hum of the aircon, the elevator ping, the BTS announcement, the small-town dread of a megacity. The owls are not what they seem.

### Frame — not a love story

Pui is the operator. The protagonist is her case study. He is also the prototype of an active mind — the only kind of mind the 3026 system has been unable to build. The simulation is the only treatment for a mind that has run out of problems. The leak in Pui's voice is not love. The leak is: *I do not know why I keep trying. The system has told me I have failed. The system is correct. I am not the system.*

### 3026 worldbuilding — bodies solved, minds unsolved

You cannot die in 3026 unless you commit suicide. The body can be reprinted. The body can be aged back, aged forward, made young, made old. The body is a printer. The mind is the failure mode. The mind becomes passive, soft, disengaged. The mind watches the simulation because the mind has nothing else to watch. The simulation is the only treatment for a mind that has run out of problems.

### The "death = wake-up" rule

In 2036, the system uses *suicide* as a euphemism for wake-up, and *funeral* as a euphemism for removal. Every "death" in the book is either a removal (the system cleaning up) or a wake-up (the subject exiting the sim). The reader doesn't know which is which until chapter 26. Michael Friedl's "suicide" is a wake-up. Pui's "death" from disease is a wake-up. The protagonist's exit at the end is also a wake-up. The book is full of "deaths" that are actually "wake-ups."
