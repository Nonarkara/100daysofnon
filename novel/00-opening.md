# Novel — Opening

This is a novel.

Set in Bangkok, 2026. The author is writing this in August. What happens between now and December is unknown. That is the first lie the novel tells you. It is also the first truth.

The novel is not real. None of the people in it are real. The street names, the apartment prices, the way a man orders his coffee on a Wednesday — invented. You are reading words.

It is, however, accurate. That is the only promise the novel makes.

The protagonist is thirty-eight. He works in AI. His rent in Bangkok is 22,000 baht a month for a one-bedroom. He has 1.2 million baht in a Thai index fund he checks every morning at 7:14 a.m. He has not called his mother in eleven days. He has a girlfriend in Da Nang he has not visited in four months.

By any reasonable measure, he is fine.

He is not fine.

The novel is about the not-fine part. The novel will not explain what that means. It will show you. It will tell you it is showing you. Then it will show you again.
