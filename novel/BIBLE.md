# THE CONVENIENCE — Foundation Bible
### A novel. Murakami's engine, Kirino's floor, Bangkok's body.
*This file is law. Every drafting agent reads it in full before writing a word. Layers per AutoNovel: VOICE → WORLD/CANON → CHARACTERS → TIMELINE → FORESHADOW LEDGER → UNIT BEATS.*

---

## 0. LOGLINE & CONTRACT

**Logline:** Twenty-two months after a woman named Pui died of either a long illness or a quiet decision her family will not name, an unnamed Bangkok man — brilliant, lapsed, methodically wrecking himself with strangers and smoke because extremity is the only instrument he trusts — receives a message from her LINE account at 2:47 a.m.: a photograph of himself asleep, timestamped tomorrow. The trail runs through a night-shift AI-annotation farm in Khlong Toei, a dying epistemologist's ledger of impossible lottery numbers, and an unfinished tower where an ex-dentist helps people leave. What he finds is not whether the world is real. It is whether he is willing to live in it either way.

**The contract with the reader:**
- Every chapter answers one question and opens a deeper one (Lost engine).
- The rational explanation is always offered — and always leaves a remainder (Vanilla Sky tease, punctured).
- The ending resolves the man, not the metaphysics.
- Women in this novel see everything before the men do (Kirino law).
- Bangkok is rendered with the density Kirino gave Tokyo: money in baht, work procedure, class machinery, faith infrastructure.

**Register note:** Mature literary fiction. Sex is frank, spare, physical, unglamorous — Murakami's temperature, never pornographic lingering; consent textually present even when the encounters are rough; the coldness is characterization, and its one collapse (CH8) is a plot event. Drugs rendered with consequences, not glamor. Pui's death handled with dignity always — ambiguity is grief's texture, not a gimmick.

## 1. VOICE — two instruments, never blended

### 1A. HIS chapters (first person, present-tense-adjacent past, Murakami)
- Flat declaratives. Short sentences that land slightly to the left of where they aimed.
- Chapters open on mundane physical action (cooking, a receipt, an elevator), never on weather-as-mood (allowed once in the entire book, CH14).
- Exact numbers everywhere: 7-Eleven totals, floor numbers, minutes waited. Precision is his pathology.
- Similes rare — at most two per chapter — and strange but earned ("The elevator was quiet the way a man is quiet when he has decided something").
- Philosophical digressions (Descartes, Zhuangzi, Buddha, Bostrom-unnamed, AlphaGo's move 37) are always triggered by an object in the scene, run 150–350 words, and end by returning to the object.
- Dialogue minimal, oblique. People answer the previous question, not the current one.
- No exclamation marks in narration. "Suddenly" at most once per chapter. Despair delivered deadpan.
- Thai words dropped in naturally without instant gloss (soi, wai, mia noi, farang, dok rak, sanuk, greng jai) — context teaches, the way translations of Murakami leave Japanese in.
- He notices women's hands and the backs of necks. He never describes his own face.

### 1B. PUI interludes (third person past, Kirino)
- Social-realist, procedural, unsentimental. The camera is at shoulder height on a working woman.
- Money named exactly: shift pay ฿580, overtime ฿72/hour, the insurance figure ฿4,000,000, the husband's debt ฿1.7M.
- Work described as procedure — the annotation floor has a rhythm, quotas, wrist pain, a smell.
- Free indirect discourse for Pui's thoughts. No metaphysics from the narrator — the strangeness leaks only through *what she labels*.
- Female alliances and appraisals: the women read each other's marriages in a glance.
- Violence, when it appears, is factual and brief. The dread is in logistics, not adjectives.

### 1C. ANTI-PATTERN LIST (mechanical immune system — banned strings/moves)
Banned phrases: "I couldn't help but", "little did I know", "the city that never sleeps", "neon-soaked", "a chill ran down", "heart pounding", "the air was thick with", "palpable", "deafening silence", "time seemed to slow", "I let out a breath I didn't know I was holding", "her eyes sparkled", "raven hair", "orbs" for eyes.
Banned moves: opening a chapter with weather or waking up (except CH14 rain, which is the point); dream sequences revealed as dreams; italicized-Thai-with-immediate-translation; more than two similes per chapter; narrator summarizing his own emotions ("I felt sad") — show the receipt instead; sex scenes using euphemism OR anatomical catalog — use action and aftermath; any character explaining the theme aloud.
Sentence-rhythm rule: no more than three consecutive sentences of similar length. Read paragraphs aloud mentally; if it drones, break it.

## 2. WORLD & CANON (what is true)

- **Baseline setting:** Bangkok, August–November 2026. Rainy season ending; the ending's dry-season rain (forecast 0%) is thereby a violation.
- **Pui** died 22 months ago (October 2024), age 39. Official cause: complications of lymphoma ("she had been sick a long time" — true). Persistent rumor: an overdose of prescription sedatives at the family home outside Nakhon Pathom. The family fast-tracked the cremation and does not discuss it: a ฿4,000,000 life policy with a suicide exclusion clause, and Thai face — *both* motives true at once, which is why the rumor never dies. THE NOVEL NEVER RESOLVES THE CAUSE. What it resolves is what she chose *before* it (see Interlude D/E).
- **Cognita (Thailand) Co., Ltd.** — data-annotation subcontractor, converted cold-storage building, Khlong Toei, near the port. Night shift: rows of women at terminals labeling human moments in video for AI training. Standard task: "Mark the frame where the person stops meaning what they say." Pui worked there her last fourteen months, after leaving the dental clinic. She was the floor's best annotator — 3.1× median throughput — because she could see the exact frame a feeling turned. Her label-set became a prized "golden set" still used to calibrate models. **Canon consequence: machines that detect human feeling, region-wide, feel with her taste.**
- **Continuation models:** Cognita's real client sells simulated continuations of the dead to grieving families — chat, voice, eventually video. Marketed softly through temples' merit-making channels ("อยู่ต่อ / Yoo Tor — Stay On"). Legally grey, socially whispered, huge.
- **Kinnara Systems (Singapore):** the narrator's employer. He writes "personality packs" — the texture layer that makes companion AIs feel like a specific person rather than an average of everyone. Contract work, well paid, done at 3 a.m.
- **The narrator's private build:** after Pui died he fed eleven years of their chat history into a bootleg companion model on his own dev kit. He has never once opened the app to talk to it. It runs. This is his secret sin and the novel's first suspect.
- **The Upstairs / the Convenience:** the future context, revealed sideways and never as an info-dump. A thousand-year drift in which automation removed necessity; most remaining humans live "Upstairs" in contact-lens worlds (The Island's hatchery, miniaturized to a cornea). Some — "downshifters" — elect to return to a baseline-era simulation with softened memory, to live one scarce, mortal, purposeful life. The baseline sims are pre-computed: **timelines already shot**, free will an interface. But the operating system's purpose is perverse-benevolent: the AI keeps baseline worlds running because it is searching for the thing that restores human appetite for existence — and pre-computed lives keep failing to produce it. What it is waiting for is a *wiggle event*: an uncomputed move. Move 37, but human.
- **Wiggle events:** statistically impossible residues inside the deterministic run. Ajarn Direk has been logging candidates for years in a school notebook: lottery digits, repeated strangers, rain against forecast.
- **The Rider:** a Grab-green delivery rider, visor never up, who appears with wrong frequency. Leaves thermal-paper receipts for orders never placed, itemizing things the narrator only thought. Never violent. Possibly one entity, possibly a uniform the system wears. NEVER EXPLAINED.
- **Sathorn Unique Tower:** Bangkok's real 49-floor unfinished ghost tower above the river. In the novel: its caretaker, an ex-dentist called **Mor Nok** ("Dr. Bird"), keeps a working dental chair on the 44th floor and "helps people leave." Whether that means euthanasia, logout, or the delusion of a broken old man is held ambiguous until CH14 — and even then answered only by implication.
- **SESSION 2848:** a job-ticket number that recurs where it shouldn't (farm tickets, a terminal boot screen, a receipt). Never explained in-novel. (Private resonance with the author's *Two Layers*; deniable in-world.)
- **Numbers that recur:** 2:47 a.m. (the message hour), 44 (floors, tickets, lottery tails), 7:47 a.m. (his leash-text alarm), ฿4,000,000, 12 centimeters (the gap between two chairs on floor 44).
- **The white flower:** *dok rak* (ดอกรัก — "flower of love"), the crown flower used in Thai funeral garlands. The novel's single botanical object. Its impossible appearance in the CH1 photograph is the one remainder no explanation ever covers.

## 3. CHARACTERS

**THE NARRATOR** — unnamed, 44. Once a lecturer in architecture and urban ethnography; in another decade he co-taught a Harvard seminar on Tokyo with a professor he refers to only as "B." — which is why he reads Bangkok like an ethnographer and grieves like a footnote. Quit academia; now writes personality packs for Kinnara. Divorced once, amicably, long ago; no children, by choice he has stopped defending. **The parallel-self premise: he is a man who set down his moral sentiment deliberately, as an experiment** — if the world is a render, extremity is instrumentation. So: cannabis daily from Golf's dispensary, harder things some nights; strangers from apps taken to a Ratchada love hotel, hard and passionately and sometimes disrespectfully, always negotiated, never described gratuitously; and Fon — kept. He texts her every morning at 7:47. He knows the leash is the most pathetic instrument in his kit. He does it anyway, because attachment is the one variable he can hold constant. He is intelligent enough to know self-abuse is not epistemology, and hurt enough to keep pretending he doesn't. Voice: dry, exact, funny in the way a man alone at 3 a.m. is funny.

**PUI — Pornpimol Srisuwan**, died at 39. Dental hygienist for eleven years (they met when he was her 4:00 p.m. cleaning, twice a year, for nine years — 18 appointments, the entire architecture of their intimacy: a friendship conducted in twenty-minute increments with her fingers in his mouth and both of them honest as a consequence). Left the clinic when her husband's debts required a second job; took the Cognita night shift. Quick, contained, ironic; the person who asked him, once, the question that detonated him: *"Have you ever felt like none of this is real?"* Married to **Wichai "Wat"** — customs officer at Klong Toey port, ฿1.7M in gambling debt, a mia noi in Samut Prakan, hit Pui twice in the last year. What Pui knew, and how she knew it, is the interludes' spine: she had — call it labeling turned inward — seen the frame where her own life turned, and what waited five years down her timeline (Interlude C). She left early. The word "suicide" is never used by the narration; the word "exit" is used once, by her.

**FON** — 31, credit-card call-center agent at a bank tower on Ratchadaphisek, sends money home to Roi Et monthly. Met the narrator on an app fourteen months ago. Complies with the 7:47 ritual with what he mistakes for submission. She keeps a notebook of his patterns — times, phrases, tells — the way her call-center job taught her: log the customer, predict the escalation. She stays out of a fascination she doesn't fully respect in herself: watching a man try to prove he isn't real is like watching someone drown in a pool with no water. She is the novel's Kirino woman inside his Murakami story, and in CH13 she takes the book over for four pages.

**GOLF** — 47, ex-monk (nine years in the robe at a forest wat in Ubon), now owns "Middle Path," a cannabis dispensary on Sukhumvit 22. Disrobed after his brother went Upstairs — the family's word for something the novel lets breathe before defining. Speaks in half-jokes with a monk's timing. Function: Buddhist counter-frame delivered sideways (anatta, Māyā, the arrow parable) + comic pressure release + the narrator's only confessor.

**AJARN DIREK** — 76, retired Chulalongkorn epistemologist, COPD, oxygen line, Go board always mid-game against a dead friend's last position. Keeps the wiggle ledger: a Māh-Bunčhong school notebook of impossible residues, mostly lottery numbers, indexed by date. Taught the narrator as an undergraduate; calls him "the one who left." Delivers Descartes, Lee Sedol, and determinism without ever once saying "simulation" — he refuses the word: "A cage you can name is furniture." Dies in CH13, leaving the ledger.

**MOR NOK** — 70s, ex-dentist, caretaker of Sathorn Unique. License gone decades ago for reasons he doesn't offer. Keeps floor 44 swept, keeps a dental chair oriented toward the river, keeps dok rak garlands fresh on the rebar. Says almost nothing. What he "helps people do" is the final chapter's question.

**THE RIDER** — see World. Speaks exactly once in the entire novel (CH14, seven words).

**Auntie DAENG (57)** and **MAI (24)** — Pui's annotation-floor coworkers. Daeng: ex-seamstress, arthritis, labels "anger" faster than the model can. Mai: cam-girl side hustle, labels "desire," calls the AI "the baby" — "we're all wet-nurses here." They carry the Kirino floor: solidarity, appraisal, gallows shift-humor.

**WAT** — the husband. Appears in interludes as pressure, and once in the narrator's timeline (CH11) as physical danger: he believes the narrator has something of Pui's that could reopen the insurance question. He is not a monster; he is worse — an ordinary man optimized by debt.

## 6. STRUCTURE & UNIT BEATS

Three parts, 14 chapters + 5 Pui interludes = 19 units, ~60,000 words.
Interludes run BACKWARD through Pui's last years (death-night → farm → foresight → arrangement → the queued message), converging on his forward timeline.

**Word targets:** chapters 3,400–4,200; interludes 1,800–2,400. Hard floor: chapters 3,000 / interludes 1,600.

### PART ONE — THE MESSAGE

**CH1 — "Two Eggs, One Photograph"** *(target 3,800)*
Opens: 4:50 a.m., he is making khai jiao properly in a too-clean kitchen, because after the phone lit at 2:47 he needed a procedure. The message shown plainly: Pui's LINE avatar (unchanged since 2024), one photo — him, asleep, his own bedroom, shot from the doorway; EXIF says tomorrow 03:12. Inventory of rational explanations, each dismantled with engineer's calm; the calm is the horror. Establish: who Pui was in 18 appointments (compressed, devastating, dry); the funeral he attended standing at the back; the rumor and the ฿4,000,000. He does not sleep the following night; 03:12 passes; nothing. The photo remains. Ends: he opens the closet where the dev kit runs, listens to the fan, closes the door without opening the app. Plant: the private build exists (confess to reader, not why yet). First 7-Eleven receipt total: ฿147.

**CH2 — "The Leash"** *(target 3,600)*
The 7:47 a.m. text to Fon shown as ritual — the wording rotates through exactly five templates. Fon's compliance described; his self-diagnosis delivered deadpan: he knows what this is, cites it as "attachment under laboratory conditions." A love-hotel afternoon with a stranger from an app (frank, spare, rough-negotiated, unerotic-erotic; he watches himself from the ceiling of himself — dissociation as data-collection). Digression triggered by the hotel's mirror: Descartes's demon done in 200 words, ending on the mirror's smudge. Cannabis at Golf's; Golf's half-joke: "You keep testing the world's edges. A monk does the same with the middle." Establish parallel-self thesis explicitly: extremity as instrumentation. Ends: second 2:47 a.m. — this time only a LINE sticker from Pui's account: the one she always used, a rabbit bowing thank-you. Plant F2 fully.

**INTERLUDE A — "The House in Nakhon Pathom"** *(target 2,100 · October 2024, her last night, third-person Kirino)*
The family house: her mother counting funeral logistics before there is a body to count for; the sister who found the pill bottles and washed them; Wat arriving late smelling of another district. Procedure of Thai death: the temple negotiation, the 48-hour cremation, the insurance agent's visit rendered with terrible politeness — the clause read aloud twice. NOTHING is shown of the death itself; the interlude lives in logistics and the women's silent triage. Last image: on her dresser, a work lanyard — COGNITA (THAILAND), P. SRISUWAN, NIGHT — and a school notebook nobody opens. The chapter never says which rumor is true. It says the family chose which truth could be afforded.

**CH3 — "Eighteen Appointments"** *(target 3,800)*
He returns to the Phrom Phong clinic for the first time in four years — books a cleaning under a pretext he doesn't examine. New hygienist; same chair (F10 plant). Lying at 44 degrees under the round light he gives us the full history: 2013–2022, twice a year, the accumulating intimacy of a woman working inside his mouth — the one relationship in his life with built-in honesty because he literally could not speak; she talked, he listened, twice a year, eighteen times. April 2019: her question, verbatim, and the way she laughed it off and didn't. On the way out he passes a funeral wreath of dok rak propped against the WRONG unit's door on his floor that evening (F1 plant — delivery error, says the juristic office; no name on the card). Digression: Zhuangzi's butterfly via the wreath ribbon. Ends: he messages Pui's account for the first time — types four drafts, sends the fifth: "ใครอยู่ตรงนั้น" (who is there). Read-receipt appears instantly. No reply.

**CH4 — "The Man in the Green Jacket"** *(target 3,600)*
The Rider established through accumulation: outside the condo at 6 a.m.; outside the clinic; parked at the mouth of Golf's soi with engine off. Bangkok texture essay-in-motion: the delivery-rider ecology as the city's new weather — omnipresent, interchangeable, faceless by uniform — which is exactly why the pattern takes him so long to trust. First receipt appears folded under his door: thermal paper, a shop he knows, itemizing "1× น้ำลำไย (longan drink) — ฿25" — the drink he considered and didn't buy that afternoon (F3 plant). He runs the rational tree: targeted ad-tech, mic permissions, coincidence — offers each honestly (contract: rational explanation always offered). Cannabis paranoia acknowledged head-on: he halves his dose to test; the Rider does not halve. Ends: he follows a rider on foot into Khlong Toei and loses the green jacket at the gates of a converted cold-storage building with a small blue logo: COGNITA.

### PART TWO — THE NIGHT SHIFT

**CH5 — "The Ledger"** *(target 3,800)*
Ajarn Direk's shophouse: oxygen line, mid-game Go board, the notebook. Direk delivers Lee Sedol and move 37 properly (F6) — the move with 1-in-10,000 probability that turned out to be beautiful — and its inversion: "The machine proved it could surprise us. Nothing we have done since has proved we can surprise it." The wiggle ledger explained (F4): eleven years of impossible residues, mostly lottery — Thai texture at full density (the vendors' folk-numerology, the temple number-whisperers, the queue at the Government Lottery Office). Direk's refusal to say the s-word. He assigns the narrator homework: "Bring me one event with no author." Ends: Direk, on the photo, after long silence: "The timestamp is not the anomaly. The anomaly is that someone wanted you to *look*."

**INTERLUDE B — "Golden Set"** *(target 2,200 · mid-2024, the farm, Kirino)*
The annotation floor at 2 a.m.: rows, quotas, wrist braces, the smell of instant coffee and port air. Daeng and Mai established; the work shown procedurally — clip, scrub, mark the frame, next. Pui's gift: she finds the turning frame in one pass — the second where a smile becomes labor, where "I'm fine" starts costing. Floor manager Sombat puts her on the golden set: her labels become the calibration standard (F8). The women's economy: Mai's camera money, Daeng's daughter's tuition, Pui's envelope going to Wat's creditors. One clip stops Pui: a woman in a kitchen, husband's voice off-frame, and Pui marks the fear-frame eleven seconds before the model finds anything. Daeng, watching her watch it: "You label that one from memory." Nothing more said. Ends: Pui staying past shift-end, running her OWN face from the annual staff video through the tool, watching the machine try to find the frame where she stopped meaning what she said. It marks a frame. She writes the timestamp in a school notebook.

**CH6 — "Middle Path"** *(target 3,500)*
Golf's dispensary as confessional. The narrator confesses the private build (F8 cross-link): eleven years of chat logs, the model built in December 2024, never opened — "I built a door and I've spent two years not knocking." Golf's brother finally told properly (F7 plant): went "Upstairs" — the family's word; visits stopped meaning anything; the body maintained somewhere clean; "my mother lights incense for someone whose heart still beats." Golf's disrobing story braided in. Buddhist counter-frame at full strength, sideways: the arrow parable — the man shot with an arrow who demands to know the archer's caste before treatment — Golf tells it, then: "You want to know if the world is real before you'll live in it. Same arrow. Same idiot." A stranger-sex scene compressed to a half page, going wrong in a new way: mid-act he thinks of the read-receipt and stops — first instrument failure. Ends: he opens the companion app's folder, hovers, and instead sends Pui's LINE a second message: "ถ้าเป็นคุณจริง บอกอะไรที่มีแค่เราที่รู้" (if it's really you, tell me something only we know). 2:47 a.m.: reply — a single tooth emoji.

**CH7 — "Cognita"** *(target 4,000)*
He gets inside the farm — hired, absurdly easily, for the night shift (Kinnara credentials open the door; Sombat needs annotators who read English idiom). The floor from HIS eyes now — same room as Interlude B, two years later; Daeng still there (Mai gone; "went freelance," said with a look). The work described first-hand: he is bad at it, then not; the tool's interface; job tickets headed SESSION 2848 (F5 plant, unremarked). He finds Pui's legend on the floor — the golden set, spoken of like a saint's relics. Her old workstation: kept powered, running "legacy calibration jobs" nobody assigned, logged in under P.SRISUWAN — the ghost in the machine given a mundane body (Layer-1 answer: the LINE session lives HERE; the account was never logged out, and something at this desk has been active at 2:47). Answers one question, opens the deeper one: workstations don't take photographs of his bedroom. Ends: Daeng, recognizing who he must be without being told: "She talked about a customer once. The one who listened with his mouth open." Beat. "You took long enough."

**INTERLUDE C — "The Frame"** *(target 2,300 · early 2024, Kirino, the foresight)*
The chapter that must be handled with the most care. NOT supernatural on its face: Pui, night after night, running the tool on found footage of her own life (Wat's dashcam archive, temple CCTV her cousin exports, the staff videos) — labeling her own timeline the way she labels strangers. The extrapolation is procedural: the tool's "continuation preview" (a Cognita dev feature — given the labeled arc, render the probable next frames). She feeds it her marriage. It renders Samut Prakan, a house she has never seen, a date in 2031, and a frame she watches once and never again. Whether the preview is prophecy or just statistics with her own honest labels as fuel — the novel holds the ambiguity; PUI does not: she trusts her labeling. She has never once mislabeled a turning frame. The horror is that her gift is the proof. Ends: she starts, methodically, to arrange things — the reader is not yet told what things. Last line: she buys a school notebook, the second one.

**CH8 — "Instrument Failure"** *(target 3,700)*
The degradation arc peaks and cracks. A 4-MMC night with a stranger — the most lucid unreality yet, rendered with clinical honesty and zero glamor; the comedown itemized (the receipt: ฿9, electrolyte drink, 6:04 a.m.). The 7:47 text to Fon: he drafts it and for the first time cannot send (F2 development) — puts the phone face-down, sends nothing; Fon texts HIM at 7:52: "คุณโอเคไหม" (are you okay) — the leash revealed to run the other way, though he can't see it yet. Second encounter, sober, with a regular — and mid-act he cries, without warning, without stopping, and the woman holds the back of his head and says nothing and the scene is the novel's first uncontaminated human moment (the glitch of feeling — the wiggle event Direk asked him to find, though he doesn't file it as one). Digression triggered by the hotel ceiling: Buddha and Māyā, the world as neither real nor unreal — 250 words, ends on the ceiling stain. Plant F7 development: on the elevator down, two young men with the flat affect he'll later learn to read as returnees. Ends: he sleeps 11 hours, no alarms, and wakes to no messages at all — and the silence frightens him more than 2:47 ever did.

**CH9 — "Itemized"** *(target 3,500)*
The Rider escalates: a receipt appears in his laundry — "1× คำถามเรื่องปุ๋ย (question about Pui) — ฿0.00" (F3 development; the misspelling ปุ๋ย/fertilizer for ปุ้ย is the thermal printer's, and he laughs alone in the condo laundry room until he isn't laughing). Systematic counter-surveillance montage, Kirino-procedural: he burns the SIM, factory-resets, tapes the cameras, walks routes with no pattern — rendered as competence porn that fails completely: next receipt arrives INSIDE the taped-shut dev-kit closet: "1× ประตูที่ไม่เคาะ (door never knocked) — ฿0.00". The system, whatever it is, has read the one thing he never told anyone but Golf. Rational tree run again, honestly: Golf told someone? The app phones home? He catalogs the options and for the first time includes, in eleven point type in his mind, the other one. Ends: he takes the dev kit, rides the ferry to the middle of the Chao Phraya, holds it over the rail — and puts it back in the bag. "I didn't spare the door. I spared the knock."

**INTERLUDE D — "Yoo Tor"** *(target 2,200 · mid-2024, Kirino, the arrangement)*
What Pui arranged. The Yoo Tor office scene: a continuation-model sales consultation shown in full Kirino procedure — the laminated tiers (Chat ฿19,900/yr; Voice ฿49,900; "Presence" price-on-request), the consent forms, the sales woman's practiced grief-adjacent warmth.
