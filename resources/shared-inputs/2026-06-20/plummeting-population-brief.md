# Shared input — "Plummeting Population" chart from UN World Population Prospects 2024

**Image:** `plummeting-population-un-wpp-2024.png`
**Source on the chart:** UN, *World Population Prospects (2024)*
**Title on the chart:** *Plummeting Population*

## What the chart shows (and what country)

Population trajectory from 1950 to 2100. The curve:
- **1950:** ~600m
- **1980:** ~1.0b
- **2010:** ~1.3b
- **2023:** ~1.4b — **peak**
- **2050:** ~1.0b
- **2100:** ~600m

A symmetric arc — the population in 2100 returns to where it was around 1955. The chart doesn't label the country, but the **1.4b peak in 2023** is unmistakable: this is **China**, per the UN's WPP 2024 median projection. China's population peaked at ~1.41b in 2022 and is projected to fall back to ~600–800m by century's end depending on the variant.

A few sanity checks on attribution: world population in WPP 2024 peaks much higher (~10.4b around 2086); India's peak is also ~1.4b but the decline is gentler; China is the only country whose published UN curve fits the symmetric peak-then-collapse this graphic shows. If Non has the original publication context (which outlet ran this, with what caption), it would confirm China explicitly — worth holding that as a fact-check item rather than asserting in published work.

## Lens 2 — for the Dao project (the Daoist contradiction)

This is where Non's framing lands hardest. His words: *"It tells us about the kinds of meaninglessness in civilization that we try so hard to achieve certain things, and now we're driving ourselves to depopulate."*

**This IS the Dao De Jing argument made visible.** Several chapters point at the same shape:

- **Chapter 9** (持而盈之，不如其已 — *"holding it filled to the brim is not as good as stopping in time"*) — civilizational striving overshoots its own purpose. The very things we built to ensure flourishing — wealth, technology, mobility, education, contraception, urbanization — produce the result that fewer children get born. We filled the cup until it spilled.
- **Chapter 30** (物壯則老 — *"things that reach their prime begin to age"*) — the arc shape is the universal pattern. Birth/death curves don't flatten; they peak and decline. Civilization is no exception.
- **Chapter 36** (將欲歙之，必固張之 — *"that which is to be contracted must first be expanded"*) — the population had to swell to 1.4b before the cultural and material conditions for falling-below-replacement could exist. The collapse was *enabled* by the success.
- **Chapter 76** (人之生也柔弱，其死也堅強 — *"a person at birth is soft, at death rigid"*) — populations are most vital while still young, smaller, building. The hard demographic numbers describe the soft demographic truth.

**Suggested use in the Dao e-book:** this chart belongs in whichever chapter discusses the futility-of-striving / the limits-of-attainment thread. Pair it with the Daoist alternative — *wu wei*, the small village, returning to the root. The chart doesn't argue against civilization; it argues that civilization's success contained the seed of its demographic reversal, and that the wise response is acceptance not panic.

If the e-book has an *afterword* or *coda* surface, this chart could anchor the closing argument that the 21st century is the century the Dao De Jing has been waiting to be read in.

## Lens 3 — for the SLIC index v3 project (birth rate as livability optimism)

Non's framing: *"It tells us about optimism in terms of birth rate, which we can use to compute livability."*

**This is the key insight:** a city/country where people choose to have children is making a *structural bet on the future*. Birth rate isn't just a demographic metric — it's a revealed-preference signal of how livable people *believe* their environment to be when projected forward 20–30 years.

**SLIC integration recommendations:**

1. **A "Generational Optimism" indicator** on the SLIC city-comparison surface. Total fertility rate (TFR) + 5-year change in TFR. A city with TFR rising even slightly from a low base is making a different bet than a city with TFR falling from a high base. The directional signal matters more than the absolute level.
2. **Cross-reference with the billionaire-density data (the previous SLIC input from earlier today).** The interesting cities are the off-diagonal ones again: high-birth-rate-low-billionaire (most of sub-Saharan Africa), high-birth-rate-high-billionaire (rare and important — these are the cities with both *capital and future*), low-birth-rate-high-billionaire (Hong Kong, Singapore, Seoul, Tokyo — wealth-without-future), low-birth-rate-low-billionaire (a hard place to live and a vanishing future). SLIC could surface this 2×2 explicitly.
3. **The chart itself as a "what we're heading into" macro layer** at the top of the dashboard. Mayors / ministers / SEIC stakeholders looking at SLIC need the China curve in their peripheral vision because Thailand's TFR (1.32 in recent years) is on the same downward trajectory — *roughly 20 years behind China*. A SLIC city ranked great-today on labor-market dynamism could be ranked very differently in 2050 if the labor pool is gone. Sustainability needs the demographic curve in the frame.
4. **"Cities defying the curve" sub-view.** Identify the few cities in the developed world that have meaningfully bent their TFR upward in the last decade — what did they change (housing policy / childcare / parental leave / cultural shift / immigration policy)? This is the *positive-deviance* analysis SLIC could become known for.
