# Shared input — Bangkok / Chao Phraya geological history (8,000 ya → 2,000 ya → present)

**Image:** `bangkok-chao-phraya-geological-history-8000-2000-present.jpg`
**Source:** Thai-language geological-history triptych. Three side-by-side maps with city labels: Saraburi, Ayutthaya, Pathum Thani, Nonthaburi, Bangkok, Samut Prakan, Samut Sakhon, Samut Songkhram.

## The three panels (verbatim translation)

**Panel 1 — 8,000 ปีก่อน (8,000 years ago) — "ทะเลรุกล้ำที่สุด (อ่าวอยุธยาโบราณ)" / Maximum marine intrusion (Ancient Ayutthaya Bay)**
- Sea level several meters higher than today
- Most of what is now central Thailand was open sea with mangrove forests + scattered islands
- Bangkok area was completely underwater

**Panel 2 — 2,000 ปีก่อน (2,000 years ago) — "ปากอ่าวและดินดอนขยายตัว" / Bay narrowed, delta extending**
- Coastline retreated south
- Created old beach lines, brackish-water lakes, mangroves, new land for habitation

**Panel 3 — ปัจจุบัน (Present day) — "แผ่นดินที่แม่น้ำสร้างขึ้น" / Land built by the river**
- Sediment from Chao Phraya and Mae Klong rivers accumulated for thousands of years
- Became the central plain where Bangkok stands today

**The full thesis the triptych makes in one sentence:** *Bangkok is geologically very recent — built on sediment that only existed in the last 2,000 years. The same rivers that built it are still depositing today; the same sea that covered it is still trying to come back.*

## Why this fits three projects at once

Non sent it without an explicit project tag, but the multi-project read is unmistakable. This is filed in `shared-inputs/` because it serves three different surfaces.

### Lens 1 — for SLIC (city analysis with geological time-depth)

**Bangkok's livability profile** (6 axes built today: wealth concentration, demographic optimism, time-cost of money, constraint conversion, economic-corridor membership, capital attraction velocity) **misses geological time-depth as a 7th axis: substrate-and-sea-level vulnerability.**

The triptych argues: *Bangkok exists in geological time as a brief deposit, and sea-level rise is the constant geological pressure pushing against its existence.* That's not metaphor — Bangkok is sinking ~1-2 cm/year (subsidence from groundwater extraction) AND sea levels are rising ~3 mm/year globally. The combination is real, measurable, and changes long-horizon livability scoring.

**SLIC integration:** add a *Geological / Climate-Adaptation* axis. For each city, surface (a) ground-elevation distribution, (b) subsidence rate, (c) sea-level projection at 2050 / 2100, (d) adaptation-spending per capita. Bangkok's profile becomes acutely informative when this axis is added — *wealthy-plateau-without-the-wealth-AND-vulnerable-substrate.* That's an actionable diagnosis no current Thai-policy dashboard surfaces.

### Lens 2 — for the Dao project (the impermanence of what looks permanent)

This is a **stunningly Daoist artifact.** Several chapters converge:

**Chapter 78** — *"Nothing in the world is softer or weaker than water, yet nothing is better at overcoming the hard and strong."* The triptych is Chapter 78 rendered as geological history. Water built the land where Bangkok stands, and water is patient enough to take it back. The hard-looking city is the soft-looking river's slow project.

**Chapter 25** — *"There is something undifferentiated and yet complete, which existed before heaven and earth..."* The 8,000-years-ago panel IS the *before-heaven-and-earth* of Bangkok specifically. The city's bedrock identity is its non-existence; the city is a *moment* in geological time, not a permanent feature.

**Chapter 30** — *"Things that reach their prime begin to age."* The 8,000 → 2,000 → present arc is the rise of a deposit; the next 2,000 years will be its dissolution if climate change continues. Civilization treats Bangkok as permanent; geology treats it as a passing phase.

**Chapter 76** — *"At birth a person is soft and weak; at death rigid and strong."* The young (recent) deposit is paradoxically the most vulnerable. The Daoist preference for the apparently-young extends to civilizations: humility about their own duration.
