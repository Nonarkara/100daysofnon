## Why this input matters for SLIC — a new load-bearing axis: *Counterparty Reliability*

**The article's actual defensible thesis** (separated from its Global-Times overlay):

> *In global infrastructure procurement, the winning bid is not the one with lowest cost or best technology — it is the one that correctly assessed the counterparty's institutional reliability over the multi-decade horizon the project requires.*

This is a **rigorous, defensible, non-partisan** framing that generalizes cleanly. And it is *exactly* what SLIC's Wharton-aligned QoL benchmarking ([[Input 09]]) and Non's methodology critique ([[Input 13]]) together imply for national-level assessment work: **the same country can score high on numerical livability metrics AND be a low-reliability counterparty for a 50-year infrastructure engagement.** These are different questions.

Counterparty Reliability sub-metrics:

- **Land acquisition track record** — % of publicly-announced infrastructure projects that complete land acquisition within announced schedule
- **Contract stability** — frequency of mid-project scope changes, vendor swaps, price renegotiations
- **Sovereign / political continuity for the project horizon** — how many PM cycles / election cycles the project must survive
- **Blame-assignment history** — pattern of assigning fault to foreign contractors when accidents occur
- **Regulatory transparency** — whether procurement decisions are auditable

**This is a SLIC methodological contribution** — the axis is not (yet) standardly measured by Wharton / Numbeo / Mercer / EIU. SLIC v3 shipping it first is a distinctive-positioning move.

## What this input DOES NOT do

- ⚠️ **This is not a claim that India is a bad counterparty in general.** India is a major, rising, complex economy; the Mumbai-Ahmedabad case is one procurement engagement among many. Counterparty-Reliability as a SLIC axis is a *per-project* + *per-decade* assessment, not a national judgment.
- ⚠️ **This is not a claim that Japan is a bad infrastructure exporter.** Japan has successfully exported HSR technology to Taiwan (2007, operating well) and has had partial success in the UK, Turkey, and elsewhere. Mumbai-Ahmedabad is one failure in a large portfolio.
- ⚠️ **This is not a claim that China's approach is superior.** China's own HSR export portfolio has significant failures (Mexico 2014 cancellation, Venezuela stalled, Jakarta-Bandung 2× over budget). The article's Global Times overlay conveniently omits this.
- ⚠️ **The "Hideki Makihara X post" claim is the most important verification bottleneck.** If real, it is a genuinely material admission. If not, the article's opening premise weakens significantly.
