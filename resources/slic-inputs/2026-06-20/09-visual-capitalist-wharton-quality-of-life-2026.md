## Why this is the most important external benchmark input SLIC has ever received

SLIC v3 was designed to be an **internal Thai livability instrument.** This Wharton index gives SLIC — for the first time — a **defensible external comparator** with:

- Rigorous methodology (Wharton is one of the world's top-3 credibility anchors in economics/business social-science)
- Global coverage (85 countries) *and* the specific regional peers Thai stakeholders care about (Japan, S.Korea, China, ASEAN)
- Recent data collection (Dec 2025 – Feb 2026 — exactly current)
- Fresh publication in 2026, matching SLIC v3's release window

**This is the country-level score that SLIC's city-level scores nest inside.** Thailand at 32/100 means Bangkok's SLIC score can be *higher* than 32 (major cities typically outperform their country averages on livability), but it *cannot* justifiably be reported at 70+ without extraordinary evidence — because doing so would put Bangkok above Japan-country (73), Australia-country (88), and every G7-except-U.S. — which would be visibly implausible to any sophisticated reader.

**SLIC v3 must acknowledge this ceiling in its calibration.** This is a Honesty-Layer discipline (same shape as [[Horizon Input 58 — Manabu]] applies for Horizon): the index must be defensible to skeptical Wharton-reading audiences, not merely internally consistent.

## Direct SLIC v3 implications

### 1. Add a "global-index-benchmark" axis to SLIC v3

Currently v3 tracks 7 internal axes (wealth concentration, demographic optimism, time-cost of money, constraint conversion, economic-corridor membership, capital attraction velocity, geological/climate vulnerability). **Add an 8th: External Global-Index Alignment** — a rolling comparison of SLIC city scores against Wharton, Mercer, Numbeo, EIU Global Liveability, and U.N. The axis does not *change* SLIC methodology; it *audits* it. Any SLIC city-score that would rank above the country's global-peer band gets flagged for extra justification.

## What this input DOES NOT do — Honesty Layer bounding

- ⚠️ **Wharton's methodology is perception-based** (15,131 adults' subjective judgments). SLIC v3 uses *observational* data (geological, economic, demographic). These measure related but not identical constructs. Do NOT conflate.
- ⚠️ **A 33-country survey population extrapolated to 85 countries** carries inherent extrapolation error. Numbers should be treated as *directional*, not precise.
- ⚠️ **"Quality of Life" as a Wharton construct is a Western-liberal-democratic-consumer framing.** It undervalues traditional / religious / kinship-based welfare structures. Thai QoL as *Thais* would measure it internally may not match Thai QoL as Wharton respondents (Thai and non-Thai) do. This is a real philosophical challenge SLIC v3 should engage, not sweep away.
- ⚠️ **Ukraine at 0 is a wartime datapoint**; it is not a livability score in peacetime terms. The index's *dynamic-range calibration* is influenced by outliers.
