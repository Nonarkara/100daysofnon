## Non's critique, unpacked into three named claims

### Claim 1 — the *ease-of-ranking* selection effect

Global perception surveys can measure only what respondents can answer briefly. Deep phenomena that require multi-week diaries, clinical instruments, or embedded ethnography **cannot enter the dataset** — not because they don't matter, but because the survey instrument can't hold them. The published index therefore *systematically* over-weights easy-to-answer dimensions and *systematically* under-weights hard-to-answer dimensions.

**This is a selection bias, not a preference bias.** No amount of respondent diversity fixes it. Only a different *methodology* fixes it.

### Claim 2 — the *developed-country image* halo effect

Respondents in a global perception survey answer partly from personal experience and partly from *received cultural imagery* about countries they have never visited. Sweden's 100.0 is not entirely 15,131 people's direct Swedish-life experience; it is significantly the *composite image* of Sweden that circulates in global media (Ikea, Nordic design, Volvo safety, hygge, gender equality). **The image is real and it earns Sweden real capital-attraction and immigration inflows. But the image and the lived reality diverge.**

Non's specific example: countries with the "image of developed" score well *even if* their lived-experience mental-health and work-stress conditions are punishing.

### Claim 3 — the *what's not mentioned* class

Non names two specific missing dimensions: **mental health** and **work stress**. The class of what's missing is much larger. A partial list:

- **Mental health** — mood disorder prevalence, anxiety disorder prevalence, treatment access, treatment stigma
- **Work stress + working conditions** — hours worked, commute time, night-shift prevalence, unpaid overtime, karoshi-type mortality, burnout diagnostics
- **Loneliness + social isolation** — % single-person households, % without a close confidant, % elderly living alone
- **Meaning + purpose** — measured by instruments like the Purpose in Life test; often collapsed with life-satisfaction but conceptually distinct
- **Existential + spiritual dimensions** — religious/traditional/community-based welfare that Western-liberal-democratic survey framings systematically undervalue (already flagged in [[Input 09]])
- **Ecological + intergenerational dimensions** — the QoL cost future generations will pay for the current generation's consumption levels
- **Freedom from surveillance / autonomy** — currently reduced to broad "civil liberties" scores that miss ambient surveillance, dark-pattern coercion, algorithmic personalization asymmetries
- **Time sovereignty** — the extent to which a person controls their own daily and weekly time (vs having it controlled by employer, algorithm, family obligation, or debt-service)

## The empirical evidence Non's critique lands hard on

Non's specific two dimensions (mental health + work stress) are the *most-documented* misses because there is rigorous parallel data that punctures the QoL ranking:

| Chart says | Parallel data says |
|---|---|
| **Japan 73.4** — top-15 QoL | *Karoshi* (過労死) is a formally-recognized cause of death; Hikikomori estimated ~1M+; suicide historically among OECD's worst; gender-inequality index near the bottom of developed world; ~40% of workers report "significantly stressed" (MHLW annual survey) |
| **South Korea 51.7** — upper-mid QoL | **Highest suicide rate in OECD** (28+/100k); *hagwon* + *n-포* culture; total fertility rate ~0.78 (world's lowest); Ministry of Employment reports rising burnout-related claims |
| **Sweden 100.0 / Denmark 98.2 / Finland 92.4 / Norway 92.4** — top-4 | Historically high antidepressant prescription rates (OECD data); seasonal-affective-disorder prevalence with polar-night season; Finland's own decades-long anti-suicide campaign was necessary precisely *because* Finnish rates were among world's worst |
| **Netherlands 90.8 / Germany 82.9** — top-10 | Dutch government + German AOK health-insurance data show *rising* chronic burnout classifications; "Burnout" is a formal medical diagnosis in Germany with rising prevalence |
| **Australia 87.5** — top-10 | Beyond Blue: 1 in 5 adults with a mental-health condition in any given year; rising youth mental-health-service load |
| **U.S. 48.6 — "fallen 10 spots since 2018"** | This is *exactly* what deaths-of-despair (Case & Deaton) + opioid crisis + rising loneliness + declining life-expectancy show. Wharton's methodology *partially* captures this trend — the 10-spot fall — but still ranks the U.S. above most of Asia and Latin America despite worse mental-health indicators than several of those countries. |

**Non's critique is empirically grounded.** The alternative-data sources (WHO Mental Health Atlas, OECD Better Life Index's work-life-balance sub-index, IHME Global Burden of Disease for mental disorders, Gallup World Poll's Cantril Ladder + daily-affect measures, deaths-of-despair literature) are all rigorous and all tell a partially different story from the Wharton leaderboard.

## The SLIC v3 methodology principle this input crystallizes

**SLIC's own methodology principle, effective this filing forward:**

> **"Any index SLIC publishes must show its own limits — what it does NOT measure — as prominently as what it does."**

Concrete implementation:

1. **Every SLIC score card ships with a mandatory "What This Score Does Not Measure" section** — same visual weight as the score itself, not a footnote.
2. **SLIC axis names should be honest about their bounds.** *"Geological/climate vulnerability (physical substrate only — does not include human adaptive capacity)"* is more honest than the shorter unqualified version.
3. **SLIC should not present rankings without accompanying uncertainty bands.** Two cities separated by 3 points are not meaningfully different at survey-noise resolution.
4. **SLIC should publish the *sources it deliberately excluded and why*.** The choice not to use Instrument X because Instrument X has bias Y is a *methodology contribution*, not a weakness.
5. **SLIC should commission ≥1 qualitative counterpart study per publication cycle** — embedded observation, focus groups, or ethnographic vignettes — to keep the survey-instrument dominance in check.

## The SLIC v3 axis this input demands be added

Add a **9th axis: "Sub-numerical Human Conditions"** — deliberately unranked, contributes to the *narrative* accompanying the SLIC score, not to the score itself. Measures:

- Mental-health burden (using WHO / IHME as primary sources)
- Work-stress burden (using ILO Working Conditions data, national labor-ministry data)
- Loneliness prevalence (using OECD SHARE + national health surveys)
- Time sovereignty (novel metric — SLIC methodological contribution opportunity)
- Meaning + purpose (Purpose in Life test scores where available)

**This axis intentionally does not roll up into a single number.** Its purpose is to be the counterweight to the aggregate scores on the other 8 axes.
