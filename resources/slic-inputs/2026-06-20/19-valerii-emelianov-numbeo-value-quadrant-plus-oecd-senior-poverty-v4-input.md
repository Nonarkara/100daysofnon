## Part A — Emelianov Value Quadrant (Numbeo, 306 cities)

### The 4-quadrant structure

The chart splits 306 cities across two axes into four labeled quadrants:

- **BEST VALUE** (upper-left) — *high quality, low cost*
- **PREMIUM LIFE** (upper-right) — *expensive but worth it*
- **BUDGET LIVING** (lower-left) — *cheap but less comfortable*
- **BAD VALUE** (lower-right) — *high cost, low quality*

### The methodological move worth naming

**Emelianov used Numbeo's QoL formula with price-linked terms REMOVED** (safety, healthcare, traffic, pollution, climate). This is a deliberately unusual choice — most Numbeo-based analyses use Numbeo's default QoL score which includes cost-of-living components, creating a circular relationship with the cost axis.

Emelianov's move (strip the price-linked QoL terms) produces an *independent* cost-vs-quality decomposition. **This is a methodological improvement SLIC v4 should study for its own composite scoring — same discipline as [[SLIC Input 13]] audit-your-instruments.**

### Cities in each quadrant — selected observations

**BEST VALUE (upper-left) — where SLIC-relevant cities land:**
- **Chiang Mai** — the only Thai city visible on the chart; sits in BEST VALUE quadrant. Confirms nomads.com [[Input 16]] Chiang Mai #10 ranking as consistent with cost-adjusted-quality reality.
- Also visible: Porto, Split, Valencia, Prague, Madrid, Abu Dhabi, Lisbon, Tokyo, Bursa, Antalya, Kaliningrad, Curitiba, Islamabad, Florianopolis, Plovdiv, Varna
- **Tokyo in BEST VALUE** is notable — contradicts common perception of Tokyo as expensive; Numbeo QoL-minus-price-terms puts Tokyo above the QoL median, and post-2020 yen weakness put Tokyo below the cost median. **Directly relevant to [[Input 70 Blair]]'s Japan frame** — Tokyo is arithmetically a *"good value"* in 2026 partly *because* of the wage stagnation and demographic contraction Blair describes.

**PREMIUM LIFE (upper-right) — the tier that "works":**
- Vienna, Eindhoven, Amsterdam, Zurich, Oslo, Sydney, Geneva, Frankfurt, Canberra, Melbourne, Tel Aviv, Vancouver, Trieste, Brisbane, Singapore, Honolulu, Berlin, Haifa, Dubai, Limassol
- **Singapore in PREMIUM LIFE** — pays $1.1M-to-retire ([[Input 18]]) but earns its cost via genuine QoL delivery

**BUDGET LIVING (lower-left) — cheap but noticeably less comfortable:**
- Tashkent, Yerevan, Guangzhou, Minsk, Tbilisi, Baku, Chisinau, Moscow, Durban, Quito, St. Petersburg, Tunis, Astana, Hyderabad, Dnipro, Yekaterinburg, Santiago, Tirana, Lviv, Montevideo, Gdansk, Jeddah, Athens, Buenos Aires, Monterrey, Belgrade, Bucharest, Turin

**BAD VALUE (lower-right) — Non's red-circled cities:**
- **San Francisco, Paris, London, New York, Los Angeles** — all in BAD VALUE, all circled by Non
- Chicago, Milan, Las Vegas, Miami, Washington DC, Hong Kong, Lyon, Dublin, Manchester, Brussels, Cleveland, Barcelona, Houston, San Juan, Naples, Detroit

**Non's red-circle is a specific analytical statement:** the West's premier "world cities" — SF/Paris/London/NYC — are all in the *high-cost / not-proportionally-high-quality* quadrant. **This is the Emelianov data point that concretely instantiates [[Input 70 Blair]]'s "the system isn't broken, it's working as designed" macro-thesis at the city-value level.**

### The devastating juxtaposition — South Korea

South Korea sits at ***#1 worst senior poverty rate in the OECD (39.8%)*** simultaneously with being a G20 economy, top-15 GDP-per-capita, home to Samsung + SK Hynix + LG + Hyundai, and Wharton QoL 52 ([[SLIC Input 09]]).

**This is [[Input 70 Blair]]'s Japan-as-preview thesis instantiated one country over.** South Korea's economic-miracle-then-collapse arc mirrors Japan's on a compressed timeline. The 39.8% senior-poverty rate is not a cultural feature; it is the *"predictable output of an incentive structure that has been allowed to run without meaningful constraint for long enough that its consequences have become the texture of ordinary life."*

Combined with [[SLIC Input 10 Goldman Korea retail margin call]] — 3.4% of Korean working-age population margin-called in a single event — the picture completes: **Korea's macroeconomic "success" masks systematic distributional failure at both ends of the age distribution.**

### Thailand is not in this dataset (OECD-member-only)

**Thailand is not an OECD member and thus is not included in this data.** But the pattern generalizes. Thai equivalents to watch for SLIC v4:

- **Elderly pension:** ~฿700/month for basic old-age pension — well below any reasonable Thai poverty line
- **Elderly labor-force participation:** Thailand has one of the highest labor-force participation rates among 66+ in Asia (per ILO data), driven by economic necessity
- **The [[Jim Carrey grandfather grounding coda]] 75-year-old FoodPanda rider is the Thai instance** of what this OECD data shows for Korea and the US at scale
- **National Statistical Office Thailand elderly-poverty surveys** are the primary Thai data source; commonly cited elderly-poverty rate ~15-25% depending on methodology

**Thai data close-out for SLIC v4** — proposal to fetch NSO + ILO Thai senior-poverty statistics and publish alongside OECD rankings. Novel Thai methodological contribution.

## Verification pass

- 🟢 **OECD Income Distribution Database** — real, authoritative, well-maintained
- 🟢 **Numbeo 306-city current database** — real (already established in prior filings)
- 🟢 **Valerii Emelianov** — data-visualization designer with a portfolio of these quadrant-style analyses on X/LinkedIn

- 🟡 **"Poverty line = below half country's median household income"** — this is the OECD-standard *relative* poverty definition. It measures within-country distribution, not absolute deprivation. **A country could have a high senior-poverty rate under this definition while still having wealthier seniors than a low-poverty-rate country's median.** Should be surfaced in any downstream use.

## What NOT to do

- ⚠️ **Do not use "South Korea 39.8%"** to dismiss South Korea as a smart-city model — Korea does many things extraordinarily well (subway, broadband, e-government, chaebol tech). The 39.8% is a specific distributional failure, not a global-model refutation.
- ⚠️ **Do not conflate "relative poverty" with "absolute deprivation"** — OECD methodology is *below half median*; a Norwegian at the 30th percentile has more real resources than a Korean at the 60th percentile. Surface the methodology in any downstream use.
- ⚠️ **Do not extrapolate Bangkok's Emelianov-quadrant position from Chiang Mai's.** Different cost structures, different QoL profiles. Bangkok's placement needs direct Numbeo close-out.
- ⚠️ **Do not treat Non's circled SF/Paris/London/NYC as opinion.** It is a data-supported observation about the BAD VALUE quadrant. Cite the Emelianov chart, not Non, when publishing downstream.
