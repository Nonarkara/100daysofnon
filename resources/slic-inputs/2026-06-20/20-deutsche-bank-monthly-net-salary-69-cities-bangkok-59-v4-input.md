### 2. The retirement-savings-vs-median-wage math — now brutal

Using [[Input 18]] cost-to-retire estimate for Thailand (~$290-350K):

**A Bangkok worker earning median professional wages would need their entire career (30+ years) to save the amount an American retiree needs to spend 15 years retiring in Thailand.** This is [[Input 70 Blair]]'s late-capitalist inequality frame reduced to one calculation.

## ⚠️ Methodology bias to name

**Deutsche Bank's data reflects professional-services / finance-industry wages, not median wages across all sectors.** The $844 Bangkok figure is likely a Deutsche-Bank-professional-band number, not a true Bangkok median. For reference:

- Thai minimum wage: ~฿357/day (Bangkok, 2024) = ~$300/month
- NSO Thailand formal-sector median wage: ~฿15,000/month = ~$450
- Central Bank of Thailand wage series: variable by sector

**The Deutsche Bank $844 is best read as "Bangkok's upper-middle formal-sector wage."** The wage-arbitrage ratios above are therefore *conservative* — a Bangkok informal-sector worker earning ~$300 competes with the same $7,984 Geneva nomad at a **26:1 ratio**, not 9.5:1.

Per [[SLIC Input 13]] audit-your-instruments discipline: surface this bias in downstream use. Better cross-references: OECD.Stat, ILO wage data, Thailand NSO Labor Force Survey.
