## Sciti-specific angle — teaching + research audience

The version of this input that lives *here* rather than at the SLIC-primary location is oriented to sciti's presumed academic-teaching audience:

### For classroom use

- **Cross-Asia housing-cost burden as a discussion prompt.** Ask students to predict Bangkok's rent-to-salary ratio *before* revealing it. Their guesses (and how those guesses cluster by student background — Thai/international, urban/rural) is a teaching-in-itself moment.
- **The Numbeo methodology as a case study in crowdsourced-data literacy.** Students should learn to *read* crowdsourced data — real signal + real bias — before they trust or dismiss it. This infographic is a clean vehicle for that lesson.
- **The Singapore infographic-glitch** (the 24% rent share ≠ $2,691/$4,291 = 62.7%) as a critical-reading exercise. *"Where does the number that made the graphic clean actually come from?"*

### For research use

- **The Bangkok data gap** as a natural research contribution. Any sciti-affiliated researcher can pull the Numbeo Bangkok numbers and publish a *"Bangkok Contribution to the Numbeo Asia Salary-vs-Cost-of-Living Comparison"* short paper. Low-cost, high-value, publication-ready.
- **Cross-reference with academic sources** — Wharton 2026 QoL Index ([[SLIC Input 09]]), Mercer Cost of Living Index, EIU Global Liveability. Numbeo has the widest coverage; the academic sources have the highest methodology confidence.

### For sciti's likely public audience (Thai policy / smart-city professionals)

- The **housing-cost-burden framing** is directly relevant to Thai smart-city policy debates around CBD densification, transit-oriented development, and affordable housing near transit.
- The **retention-constraint argument** (cities that require 40%+ of salary for rent create workforce-retention problems) is the exact conceptual bridge from cost-of-living data to smart-city talent-attraction strategy.
