## The teaching on the plaque (three panels)

**Panel 1 (top):** A piece of wood next to a bar of fine gold (label visible: *"FINE GOLD 999.9 NET 1000 g"*). Text:

> *"A piece of wood has no value compared to gold."*

**Panel 2 (middle):** Two hands grasping a piece of wood floating on the sea, with the sun on the horizon. Text:

> *"But when you are drowning, you will hold on to the wood, not the gold."*

**Panel 3 (bottom inscription):**

> *"Not everything that shines is truly valuable, and not everything that seems ordinary is worthless."*

## Why this is one of the strongest Dao De Jing teaching artifacts I've filed

The plaque renders a *contemporary visual parable* of an insight the Dao De Jing returns to in at least five chapters. The medium itself — bronze relief, weathered, sculptural, enduring — matches the message. **Form is teaching.**

### Dao De Jing chapter mappings

**Chapter 11** — 三十輻共一轂，當其無，有車之用 — *"Thirty spokes share one hub; it is the emptiness that makes the wheel useful."* The wood's apparent worthlessness (no market value) becomes its essential utility in the drowning context. Same teaching: *value isn't intrinsic, it's relational.* The plaque is Chapter 11 in a single image.

**Chapter 22** — 曲則全 — *"The bent will be whole."* Gold is "perfect / pure" in market terms; wood is "bent / ordinary." But the bent and ordinary survives where the perfect drowns. Daoist preference for the apparently-lesser.

**Chapter 76** — 人之生也柔弱，其死也堅強 — *"At birth a person is soft and weak; at death rigid and strong."* Gold is dense, hard, sinks. Wood is light, soft, floats. The soft saves; the hard kills. The plaque is Chapter 76 made visible.

**Chapter 78** — 天下莫柔弱於水 — *"Nothing in the world is softer or weaker than water, yet nothing is better at overcoming the hard and strong."* The drowning scene is set in *water*, the medium that reverses every market valuation in one slide.

**Chapter 81** — 信言不美，美言不信 — *"True words are not beautiful; beautiful words are not true."* The closing line on the plaque — *"not everything that shines is truly valuable, and not everything that seems ordinary is worthless"* — is almost a direct paraphrase of Chapter 81.

**Five chapters converge on one image.** That's not coincidence. That's why the parable lands.
