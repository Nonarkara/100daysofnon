**Source:** Long-form inspirational essay, forwarded by Non 2026-09-16 without commentary or attribution. **Not written by Non.** Voice is not his register. **Genre signature is unmistakably late-20th-century Anglo-Christian-Filipino motivational** — the *"true measure of a man,"* *"look out for number one,"* *"make success a habit,"* *"belief is the mother of reality"* voice that circulated widely in Reader's Digest, corporate motivational speaking, and Manila-Catholic-newsletter forwards through the 1990s-2000s. Attribution likely lost through decades of email-and-photocopy chain.

**Filed as Dao Input 11** — sibling to [[Dao Input 10]] (Thai Buddhist non-clinging summary). Both are wisdom-tradition inputs for the Dao De Jing e-book. Handling requires the same tradition-boundary discipline plus a register-honesty note.

## The 22 items (+3 counter-items) — structural

**Keep:** calm · chin up · spirits high · your word · in time with your inner drummer · in touch with the child in you · abreast of the times · in shape · your mouth shut · good friends · great memories not heavy objects · a diary · saying grace and thanks · on moving forward · out of danger · up the good work · young · on dancing · on loving · on dreaming · on hoping · on believing

**Do not keep:** up with the Joneses · issues and grudges · resentments and memories of painful experiences

## Register-honesty note — first-class, per today's session discipline

**The essay's voice is genre-specifically Anglo-motivational and diverges from the Buddhist/Daoist register in ways worth naming:**

| The essay's move | Buddhist / Daoist counter-move |
|---|---|
| *"Keep on believing — belief is the mother of reality"* (#22) | *"See life as it actually is"* ([[Dao Input 10]] #8); [[Input 70 Blair]] would call *"belief creates reality"* naive under structural conditions the individual cannot will away |
| *"Keep on dreaming"* + *"hope for nothing but the best and that's what you will be granted"* (#20, #21) | Buddhist *dukkha-nirodha* is achieved through *cessation of clinging*, not through hopeful pursuit; Daoist wu-wei is the *absence of willed reaching* |
| *"Make success a habit"* (#16) | Chapter 44 asks *"which is dearer, name or self?"* — "success" is precisely the name Laozi warns against making load-bearing |
| *"Look out for number one (that's you!)"* (#15) | Buddhist *anatta* — there is no permanent number-one to look out for; [[Dao Input 10]] point 4 (let go of "me" and "mine") |
| *"The true measure of a man"* (#4) | Gender-universalizing single-standard framing not present in either Buddhist or Daoist tradition |
| *"Keep on hoping — keep faith in the abundance of the universe"* (#21) | New-Thought / Prosperity-Gospel adjacent framing; both Buddhist and Daoist frames are more austere about *abundance-as-birthright* |

**This is not a takedown of the essay.** The essay's items *work* for the reader orientation it's written for — Anglo-Christian-motivational-lay-audience — and many items map to real cross-tradition wisdom. **But the *register* is aspirational-cultivation-achievement**, whereas Buddhism and Daoism are more oriented to *reducing-emptying-yielding*. Opposite spiritual gestures that arrive at overlapping practical instructions.

**Same discipline as [[Dao Input 10]]:** name the tradition boundary, preserve the honest convergences, don't silently collapse the traditions or claim the essay is what it isn't.

## Three placements for the Dao De Jing e-book

### 1. Cross-tradition appendix expansion (continues [[Dao Input 10]]'s placement)

The Dao Input 10 filing proposed a *"Where Laozi and the Buddha converge (and where they don't)"* appendix. **This input adds a third voice — the Anglo-motivational tradition** — and the appendix becomes *"Three wisdom voices on non-clinging: Laozi, the Buddha, and the modern motivational tradition. Where they converge. Where they diverge. What each register serves best."*

This is a legitimate scholarly move that also reflects reader-orientation reality — many Thai and Filipino readers encountered wisdom teachings in the motivational-Christian register before or alongside Buddhist / Daoist primary texts.

### 2. Chapter 55 opener — the child-like-virtue chapter

Item #6 (*"keep in touch with the child in you"*) + Laozi's Chapter 55 (含德之厚,比於赤子). Same shape as the [[Dao Input 10]] Chapter 44 opener. Two voices on the same teaching from different eras.

### 3. Register-diagnosis card — the *"which voice serves your reader today"* framework

Not an essay-appendix but a reader-facing card explaining *when to reach for the Buddhist/Daoist frame vs when the aspirational-motivational frame serves better.* Rough answer:
- **When the reader is in structural precarity ([[Input 70 Blair]] conditions)** — Buddhist / Daoist non-clinging frame protects; motivational frame can *add harm* by placing failure at the individual door
- **When the reader has agency and just needs energizing** — motivational frame can catalyze; Buddhist/Daoist frame can feel deflating
- **When the reader is between** — probably both, in rotation

This card is Horizon-Honesty-Layer-adjacent methodology, portable outside the Dao project.
