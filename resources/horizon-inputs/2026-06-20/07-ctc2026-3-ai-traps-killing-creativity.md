# Input 7 — Creative Talk Conference 2026: "3 AI traps that kill your creativity"

**Source:** Insightist Facebook post summarizing a Creative Talk Conference 2026 session, Ideal Stage & Insight Stage. Speakers: Geng Sitthipong Sirimaskasem (CEO Creative Talk, moderator), Aey Phattra Attanon (Jongrakdee), An Wuthisak Amornrumpon (Factory01), Pete Pongpiti Pasukyeud (Founder/CEO AD ADDICT, moderator). Hashtags: #Insightist #AI #CTC2026 #CreativeTalk.

**Verbatim summary** (Thai original preserved below; key claims in English here):

The session names **three AI traps that quietly kill creativity** in brand and agency work — each with a Factory01 / Jongrakdee case study.

### Trap 1 — Same question = same answer
If everyone prompts AI the same way, everyone gets the same output, and no one stands out. *"If everyone enters the same question, the answers won't differ. What matters is selecting and twisting the truth so it stands out."* — An.

**Case:** **Hatari (Thai fan brand) with protective yantra on fans.** Asking AI "should we do this campaign?" → AI said don't. Factory01 did the opposite, and produced it 100% analog. Result: massive conversation that AI couldn't have predicted.

**Escape:** use AI as the base, not the answer — see what the majority will get, then twist to differentiate.

### Trap 2 — AI says don't → so don't
Executives check AI quickly, see the limitations, become risk-averse, use AI as a decree-staff to reject new ideas. The brand never leaves its comfort zone. *"If they open with 'I checked with AI', I'd say goodbye and come back later."* — Aey Jongrakdee.

**Case:** **90-year-old Indonesian global brand.** Client believed statistics deeply. Factory01 proposed doing the opposite of every data point. Executives terrified at presentation. They held their breath and shipped → best response in the brand's 90-year history.

**Escape:** real courage isn't choosing the safe option; it's taking the brand outside the zone the data defines.

### Trap 3 — Believing AI will replace everything
Panic at AI-company PR. Misunderstanding always beats truth. Forgetting that execution requires being human. Ideas become commodity, while the value AI can't generate gets neglected. *"Every AI company on earth has revenue, but not profit. The money is from fundraising and PR."* — An.

**Case:** **Jongrakdee executing with their own hands.** Early small-agency days, no budget for a production house. Decided to design and produce in-house: *"if we're going to fail, let it be with our own hands."* That hand-execution capability became the moat AI cannot replace.

**Escape:** focus on execution, not just idea. AI can think of ideas; it cannot make them happen in reality.

## Why this input matters for Horizon

This is the **executive-judgment layer** that the previous six inputs don't fully cover. The Horizon assessment Non critiqued was test-shaped; this material is decision-shaped — *when to override AI*, not *how to comply with AI*. Specifically:

- It strengthens **M1 (AI as a team)** by adding the rule: a team includes knowing when *not* to listen to a member. The Hatari case is the canonical example.
- It strengthens **M3 (purpose-based assessment design)** by giving the assessment a behavioral dimension: not just *can you produce a podcast using AI tools today*, but *can you bet against AI's recommendation when your judgment says to*.
- It strengthens **M5 (frontier ethics)** with An's PR-vs-profit observation — Horizon learners should leave knowing how to read AI-company economics critically, not just be hyped by them.
- It is *not* covered anywhere in the AIS Academy curriculum (input 6) — adds to Horizon's moat.
