## The 5 named prompt-format mnemonics

Each is a 3–4-letter acronym that names the *shape* of a useful prompt for a specific class of task.

### 1. PAR — Problem · Action · Result

| Slot | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Problem** | Identify the challenge or issue |
| **Action** | Describe the steps taken to address it |
| **Result** | Highlight the outcome or improvement |

**Example:** *Problem: A website is experiencing slow load times. Action: Optimize performance by improving caching and image compression. Result: Faster load times and improved user experience.*

**Best for:** issue resolution, process improvement, customer-feedback analysis, product/service optimization.

### 2. RTF — Role · Task · Format

| Slot | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Define who the AI should act as |
| **Task** | Specify what needs to be done |
| **Format** | Define how the output should be structured |

**Example:** *Role: Act as a digital marketing assistant. Task: Create an email marketing campaign for a new product. Format: Persuasive tone with key features and a clear call to action.*

**Best for:** social-media post creation, email marketing, blog writing, product descriptions.

### 3. TAG — Target · Action · Goal

| Slot | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Target** | Define the audience |
| **Action** | Specify what you want the AI to do |
| **Goal** | Clarify the desired result |

**Example:** *Target: Small business owners. Action: Explain the benefits of cloud-storage solutions. Goal: Convince them to switch from local storage.*

**Best for:** product-benefit explanations, educational content, social-media campaigns, conversion-focused messaging.

### 4. BAB — Before · After · Bridge

| Slot | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Before** | Describe the current problem state |
| **After** | Describe the desired future state |
| **Bridge** | Introduce the solution that connects the two |

**Example:** *Before: Low productivity in remote teams. After: Highly efficient and motivated teams. Bridge: A productivity tool that enables collaboration.*

**Best for:** testimonials, product-launch stories, sales and marketing narratives.

### 5. CARE — Context · Action · Result · Evaluation

| Slot | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Provide the background information |
| **Action** | Describe what was implemented |
| **Result** | Explain the outcome |
| **Evaluation** | Assess effectiveness and impact |

**Example:** *Context: High employee turnover in a growing company. Action: Implemented an employee-engagement program. Result: Reduced turnover and improved company culture. Evaluation: Measure impact after six months.*

**Best for:** project reports, performance reviews, customer-success stories, marketing-campaign analysis.

### Why named mnemonics matter for a Thai-language learner audience

Unmemorable prompt advice doesn't transfer. *"Be specific. Give context. State the goal."* — true, but no one remembers it when they sit down to type. A named acronym (PAR, RTF, TAG, BAB, CARE) gives the learner a *hook* — they remember it, they recall it under pressure, they apply it.

This is the same pedagogy as Non's own 4P framework (Purpose / Practical / Proof / People — from the Chonburi lecture), 4C framework (Compensation / Cause / Community / Career — from the TKC workshop), and G/D/U/C (Growth / Delivery / Utilization / Communication — from the TKC matrix-org deck). **Acronyms are the unit of memorable framework transfer.** The Horizon curriculum's effectiveness depends on the learner remembering the frameworks weeks after the lesson.

### Lesson design

Build M1's prompt-engineering primitives lesson as a **5+3 grid**:

| Primitive | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Brief-first / Ask-back-first** (Input 10) | The task is novel and you need AI to interrogate you, not guess |
| **Quality Gate checklist** (Input 10) | Before accepting output as done |
| **Retrospective-after-delivery** (Input 10) | After every shipped artifact |
| **PAR** (Input 13) | Documenting an issue resolved (support tickets, postmortems) |
| **RTF** (Input 13) | Asking AI to take on a role and produce a styled output (marketing, social) |
| **TAG** (Input 13) | Persuading a defined audience toward a defined outcome (sales copy, ads) |
| **BAB** (Input 13) | Selling a transformation (case studies, testimonials, before/after) |
| **CARE** (Input 13) | Writing a structured report with measurable evaluation (project recap, performance review) |

This grid becomes the **single most-referenced page** in M1 — learners will return to it every time they sit down to draft a prompt. It deserves to be a printable single-page PDF + an interactive web page (each mnemonic expandable with an in-the-flow example).

### A test the learner can grade themselves on

Give the learner one paragraph of "bad prompting" — vague, unfocused, no structure — and ask them to rewrite it three ways using three different mnemonics. The point isn't that one is right; the point is that the *same intent* takes radically different shapes depending on which acronym you reach for. Once the learner can do that exercise, they have internalized the primitives.
