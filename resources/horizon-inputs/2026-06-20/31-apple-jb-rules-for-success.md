# Input 31 — "JB's Rules for Success" — Apple internal operating principles, 2004 employee badge

**Source:** Photo of a 2004-era Apple employee security badge (lanyard-style, Apple logo visible top-right) with **"JB's Rules for Success"** printed on the back. Caption text reads: *"A former Apple employee's badge from 2004 reveals the company's internal rules for success."* Carousel-style social post (visible *SWIPE* indicator).

**"JB"** is almost certainly **John Brandon**, who was Apple's VP of Sales / Channel Sales for many years (joined Apple in 1996, ran sales for two decades). These rules were widely encoded into Apple's sales-org culture during his tenure and have circulated in Apple employee lore ever since.

## The 11 rules (verbatim from the badge)

1. **Let go of the old, make the most of the future**
2. **Always tell the truth, we want to hear the bad news sooner than later**
3. **The highest level of integrity is expected, when in doubt, ask**
4. **Learn to be a good businessperson, not just a good salesperson**
5. **Everyone sweeps the floor**
6. **Be professional in your style, speech and follow-up**
7. **Listen to the customer, they almost always get it**
8. **Create win/win relationships with our partners**
9. **Look out for each other, sharing information is a good thing**
10. **Don't take yourself too seriously**
11. **Have fun, otherwise it's not worth it**

- **Behavioral** (not aspirational)

## What's instructive about *which* rules made JB's list

The selection itself is teaching content. Note what's NOT on the list:
- No "be the best in your category"
- No "exceed your number"
- No "outwork your competitors"
- No "be passionate about Apple"

What IS on the list:
- **Truth-telling discipline** (rules 2, 3, 9 — about information flow and integrity)
- **Customer-as-correct posture** (rule 7 — *"they almost always get it"* is a strong claim)
- **Anti-ego practice** (rules 5, 10 — *"everyone sweeps the floor"* + *"don't take yourself too seriously"*)
- **Long-horizon over short-quarter** (rule 1, 4 — businessperson > salesperson)
- **Partner-as-equal** (rule 8 — win/win, not zero-sum)
- **Joy as a non-negotiable** (rule 11 — explicitly stated)

The implicit philosophy: *if you get these 11 right, the sales numbers take care of themselves. If you optimize the sales numbers and miss these 11, the numbers eventually collapse.* This is **EGO-VOID applied to organizational design** — value the underlying behaviors, not the metric they produce.

## Two specific rules that resonate with today's Horizon themes

**Rule 7 — "Listen to the customer, they almost always get it."**

This is the *positive* version of Input 25's Invisibility Test (*"the day your customer praises your AI for being clever is the day automation failed"*). The Invisibility Test says don't impress the customer. JB's Rule 7 says *believe* the customer. Together: the customer is the one whose judgment matters, not your design choice.

**Rule 5 — "Everyone sweeps the floor."**

This is the *operational* version of the M1 + M6 multi-agent orchestration practice (Input 18) — no role is too small for the orchestrator/reviewer to do themselves. The badge's claim isn't just about humility; it's about preventing the calcified hierarchy where senior people stop touching the work. **In agent-augmented work, this becomes critical: the operator who doesn't sweep the floor doesn't notice when an agent is silently breaking things.**
