## The three data points

| # | Headline number | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **$442 billion** lost to scams worldwide | The global aggregate. For scale: that's roughly the GDP of Thailand. The losses are real and structural, not anecdotal. |
| 2 | **80%** of global consumers received a scam attempt in the last year; **29%** engaged with the outreach | Targeting is universal. One in three of those targeted *responded*. The funnel is alarmingly fat at the top. |

**Candidate addition — M7: AI-enabled scams and how to defend against them**

| Module facet | Content |
|---|---|
| **The threat surface (Lesson 1)** | The 2025 numbers (Input 15 data). Set the stakes. Use the $442b / 80% / 29% / 71% chain as the opening shock. |

## Why this module fits the broader Horizon arc, not just a side topic

The earlier curriculum brief (M1–M6) is **offensive** — how to build with AI, ship with AI, become indispensable to clients who pay you to deploy AI. That's the production side.

M7 makes Horizon **complete** by adding the **defensive** side. The same AI fluency that builds agents is the fluency that recognizes when AI is being weaponized against you or your family. **A Horizon graduate is someone who can both wield AI as a builder AND defend against it as a target.** Without M7, the curriculum is asymmetric: you teach people the offense without the defense, and your graduates are perfectly equipped to build but still vulnerable as targets.

The pedagogical move is mirror-symmetric:
- M1–M6: *"You can build this. Here's how."*
- M7: *"They can build this against you. Here's how to recognize and resist."*

Same primitives, opposite purpose.
