## The 4 principles the post argues (substance, separated from the hard sell)

1. **Tie what you do to "revenue."** If you can prove your work makes or retains money, you can ask for a raise or charge more. Examples: admin who saves cancellations becomes revenue-protector; graphic designer who lifts click-through becomes revenue-multiplier.
2. **Sell to "people with money."** $3,000 to someone earning $15k is hard; $50,000 to a company netting $10m is easy — businesses think in ROI. *B2B* in his framing includes solopreneurs (selling courses, SOPs, recipes that help others make money).
3. **Price by "value" not "time."** Hourly billing caps your income and punishes speed. Rule of thumb: charge ~10% of the value you create for the client.
4. **Build skills "close to money."** Sales + Marketing are the canonical close-to-money skills. Pivots suggested: generic graphic design → ad-graphic-that-moves-sales; generic content → conversion copywriting; generic video editing → cart-driving short-form.
