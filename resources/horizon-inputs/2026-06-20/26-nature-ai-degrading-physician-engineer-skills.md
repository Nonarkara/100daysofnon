## What the studies found (per the Nature article + cited research)

- **Physicians' colonoscopy detection skill declined** after they grew accustomed to using AI assistance for spotting pre-cancerous growths. The AI made the *current* job easier; it made the underlying skill *worse* over time.
- **77% of physicians** report worry about losing skills due to AI over-reliance (survey data cited in the article).
- **Software engineers** similarly affected (mentioned in the subtitle; specifics in the full article body — Horizon Research version should fetch the full article for the specific software-engineering studies).

A related preprint cited in the search results — [*"Automation Bias in Large Language Model Assisted Diagnostic Reasoning Among AI-Trained Physicians"*](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.23.25334280) (medRxiv, Aug 2025) — finds that physicians trained on AI-assisted diagnosis develop *automation bias*: they defer to AI recommendations even when the AI is wrong, more than physicians who haven't been trained with AI.

This is a foundational finding. **AI doesn't just take jobs (Input 11); it takes skills even from people who keep their jobs.**

Through 25 inputs the curriculum has been building toward *"AI as a team, AI as leverage, climb the four tiers and become AI-leveraged."* All true. ## The Skill Preservation principle in operational form

Concrete techniques the Horizon curriculum should teach:

1. **Deliberate practice days without AI** — periodically work entirely without AI tools on a task the AI usually does for you, so the underlying skill stays sharp
2. **Blind review** — produce your own answer first, then check what the AI says, *only after* you've committed to your own
3. **Skill audits** — quarterly test of an underlying skill you've automated, against a baseline you recorded before automation
4. **Reasoning-not-answers prompting** — ask AI to explain *why* it's recommending what it recommends, so you learn the underlying logic instead of just receiving the output
5. **Teach what AI taught you** — the act of teaching a junior consolidates the skill; if AI does it for you and a junior, you both deskill
