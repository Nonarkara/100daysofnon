# Input 14 — The Four-Tier AI-Adoption Ladder (train-metaphor infographic)

**Image:** `14-four-tier-ai-adoption-train-metaphor.png`
**Source:** Unattributed in the version Non sent (worth confirming the original creator before re-publication); the visual idiom is steam-locomotive-with-AI-leverage-stack.

**Non's framing — explicit directive:**

> *"This could serve as a foundation of tiers of skills we want people to be able to achieve in the Horizon 45 project."*

This is the **most structurally-loaded input** of the day. Non isn't suggesting an addition; he's naming a foundation. Treat the four tiers as the curriculum's **maturity ladder**.

## The four tiers as illustrated

| Tier | Name on the infographic | State | Visual idiom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | The Skilled Professional | *Capable, experienced, but **isolated*** | Person at a desk, **"Unused Potential"** label, no leverage |
| **2** | The Hesitant Solo Operator | *Pulls alone, feels overwhelmed* | One person dragging a cart manually |
| **3** | The AI-Leveraged Professional | *Reduces friction, focuses on execution* | Person on a locomotive with an "AI Assistant" and "AI Leverage Stack" |
| **4** | Consistent Impact & Growth | *Sharing knowledge imperfectly, staying visible* — guiding maxim **"Start Small, Share Imperfectly"** | Full train with "Social Proof" loops, the message **"AI helps you succeed, it doesn't replace"** |

The progression is *not just about tool adoption*. Each tier names a different limitation being broken:

- **T1 → T2:** *isolation* breaks. The professional accepts that working alone with their skills is leaving value on the table. They start to reach for help — but the help is still manual.
- **T2 → T3:** *manual labor* breaks. The operator stops pulling alone and starts using AI as the leverage stack. Friction reduces; the person focuses on the execution that only humans can do.
- **T3 → T4:** *invisibility* breaks. The leveraged professional starts sharing — imperfectly, consistently. Social proof loops compound. *Impact and growth become continuous rather than episodic.*

Each tier-break is a different skill, addressing a different psychological barrier. The framework isn't about *learning tools*; it's about *changing relationships to work*. That's why it's a foundation, not a checklist.

## How this maps onto the existing Horizon module structure

The 6-module plan from `brief.md` (M1 through M6 + 2 cross-cutters) can be **explicitly indexed against the tier ladder**:

| Tier | Tier-break being taught | Horizon module(s) that deliver it |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 → T2** | Break isolation; accept you need leverage | **M3 (purpose-based assessment design)** lands the *diagnostic* — *"here is what you could be doing that you aren't, and here is the gap";* **Cross-cutter Executive Judgment** lands the *permission* — *"the skilled professional's judgment is the moat, not the obstacle"* |
| **T2 → T3** | Break manual labor; install the AI leverage stack | **M1 (AI as a team / orchestration mindset)** + **M2-A or M2-B (agentic patterns)** + **M4 (AI in the physical world)** + **M6 (parallel agents with Cowork)** — the entire technical core of the curriculum lives in this tier-break |
| **T3 → T4** | Break invisibility; ship and share | **Cross-cutter Retrospective + Pattern Library** delivers the *cadence* — *"every project ends in a retro, every retro becomes a public artifact";* **the Pinterest-style artifact library** (Input 12 spec) delivers the *surface* — *"your portfolio is visible the moment you have something to show"*; **M5 (frontier ethics)** delivers the *credibility* — *"you can write about the field with judgment, not just hype"* |

This **inverts the design question** from *"what modules do we need?"* to *"what tier-break does each module deliver?"* Modules without a clear tier-break attachment are suspects — they're either decorative or they belong to a tier-break the curriculum hasn't named yet.

## Implications for assessment (the M3 connection)

Horizon's purpose-based assessment (M3) now has a **simple diagnostic spine**: where on the tier ladder is the learner today, and where do they want to be in 6 months?

A 5-question self-assessment captures this faster than any rubric:

1. Do you have skills that you use solo, isolated from any AI tools? (→ T1 if yes for ≥80% of work)
2. When you reach for AI, do you feel like you're still doing all the pulling? (→ T2)
3. Have you set up a *stack* of AI tools you reach for in sequence, each for what it's best at? (→ T3 if yes)
4. Do you ship and share publicly — imperfectly, consistently? (→ T4 only if yes to "publicly" *and* "consistently")
5. Has your last 3 months of public-facing work compounded — i.e., is each artifact reaching more people than the one before? (→ T4 confirmed)

The score determines which module the learner enters first. This is the **enrollment-time recommendation engine** Horizon's landing page should run.

## Implications for the recruiting frame (the Input 11 connection)

The 5-at-risk-Thai-jobs frame from Input 11 names the **threat** (you might disappear). The tier ladder names the **destination** (here's where Horizon will get you — Tier 4, sharing knowledge imperfectly and staying visible, AI helping you succeed and not replacing you). Together:

> *"Five Thai job categories may disappear in five years. The exit isn't 'use AI better.' The exit is climbing four tiers — from isolated skilled professional to consistently-shipping AI-leveraged operator with a public portfolio. Horizon is the curriculum that climbs the four."*

That landing-page copy writes itself once both inputs are in the recruiting frame.

## The "Start Small, Share Imperfectly" maxim — operating principle for the whole curriculum

The bottom-tier guidance — *Start Small, Share Imperfectly* — should be Horizon's **operating motto**, printed on the landing page, on the certificate, in every retrospective template. Every Horizon-shipped artifact is *imperfect by design* — because waiting for perfection is what keeps T1/T2 people from ever crossing into T3/T4. The maxim makes the imperfection a feature, not an apology.

This also resolves a tension in earlier inputs:
- Input 2 (Museum of Meaningless Metrics) warned against optimizing for the wrong measurements
- Input 11 (5 at-risk jobs) warned against the survival-by-perfection trap

The maxim *"start small, share imperfectly"* is the **affirmative version** of those two negative warnings. It tells the learner what to *do*, not just what to avoid.

## Add to the brief

The Horizon module brief should be updated to make the tier ladder the **first organizing frame** before the modules themselves. Suggested structure:

1. **Why Horizon** (anchored on Input 11 — the 5 at-risk jobs)
2. **The Tier Ladder** (anchored on Input 14 — the 4 tiers, the train metaphor)
3. **The Modules** (M1 through M6 + cross-cutters, each indexed to which tier-break it serves)
4. **The Pinterest Library** (Input 12 spec)
5. **Operating Motto: Start Small, Share Imperfectly**

This is a cleaner narrative for the learner than starting with the module list. The learner reads the threat → meets the ladder → finds the module that moves them up → starts shipping imperfectly.
