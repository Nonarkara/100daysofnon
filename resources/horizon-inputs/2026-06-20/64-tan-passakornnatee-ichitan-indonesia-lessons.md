## The three-lesson structure

### Lesson 1 — Indonesia: 5 years of losses, 6th year of profit

**The setup:** Tan chose Indonesia ~10 years ago for population size (~270M) + young demographics + families still marrying and having children (unlike aging Thailand). Partnered with a local convenience-chain operator with 10,000+ locations. Should have been a slam dunk.

**Three simultaneous mistakes:**

1. **Product-market fit** — the Ichitan honey-lemon green tea did not resonate. Indonesians prefer thick spiced drinks with complex flavor. (Non validated this from personal observation on multiple Indonesia trips — the drink culture there is thick, sweet, cheese/cream-heavy, spice-forward.)
2. **Geography killed unit economics** — Indonesian tea market is ~฿70B, but Indonesia is 3,000+ islands. One factory shipping nationwide meant more sales = more losses.
3. **Retail channel mismatch** — Indonesia's tea market is 80% traditional stores + 20% modern retail *by revenue*. But *by store count*, modern retail is only 5% of stores. When promotions succeeded and demand spiked, the product wasn't in the stores where the newly-interested consumers actually shopped.

**The pivot:** in year 6, Tan personally went to Indonesian supermarkets and kiosks and observed consumers buying **Thai milk tea sachets to take home.** He shifted the production line from green tea to Thai milk tea. That was the year Ichitan Indonesia became profitable.

**The generalized lesson from multi-country experience (Indonesia + Philippines + Bangladesh):** *"When doing business in a foreign country, send executives to live embedded with locals for at least 4–5 years."* Not a market-research pass. Not a business-trip series. **Lived time.**

### Lesson 2 — Do not trust research over field observation

**Tan's argument:** questionnaire respondents, friends, and inner-circle contacts lie politely — they say "delicious" out of manners. Trusting these answers leads to *~95% failure rate*.

**Prescription:** entrepreneurs must physically go and live with locals. No 5-star hotels. No private cars. Take buses, take taxis, talk with residents for *years* — because real consumer needs are like gold, only visible to those who stay lower.

**Supporting frames Tan drew on:**
- *"Dig canals during peaceful times"* — read, listen to elders, attend seminars during good times; when the storm comes, the pre-dug canals absorb the flood
- *"Cut the arm to save the body"* — 1997 Tom Yum Kung crisis lesson from elder-conversation memory: those who sold assets and took the reputational hit to pay debt quickly were the ones who recovered and grew wealthy again
- *The Godfather* lessons — don't reveal weakness in negotiations; quiet-composed people are more dangerous than loud-emotional ones; the *offer-you-cannot-refuse* principle applied when powerful buyers approached his land holdings; giving help without expecting return builds capital that returns unexpectedly

### Lesson 3 — Economy stays down 3–4 more years; asymmetric opportunity for cash-holders

**Tan's macro read:** current and next 3-4 years — no recovery. Signals: reduced buying power at street markets, factories cutting overtime, tourist arrival stats decelerating.

**Divergent playbooks depending on position:**

**For cash-rich operators** — this is opportunity. Tan is buying: Thonglor-area hotel, 160 rai of land at Khao Chi Chan, several billion baht total. Precondition: a *"small waterfall"* of stable recurring income — Ichitan's Chiang Mai hotel + Lopburi market + building leases = ฿50-100M/month cash flow before D&A and tax, 2× fixed costs. This qualifies him to borrow at 3.25% and deploy into assets yielding 8%.

**For SMB operators and individuals with debt** — opposite direction. **Sell non-income-producing assets** (gold, cars, condos, houses) to eliminate debt entirely; reset from a debt-free position. Non-income-producing assets are *liabilities*, especially with distressed condo/house inventory flooding the market.

**Hotel operating discipline** — Ichitan's Chiang Mai hotel uses **24-hour check-in / 24-hour check-out** (guest arrives at 3 AM, gets 24 hours to that time next day) + 24-hour breakfast service + free room upgrade if booked category isn't available. Product differentiation on service norms, not on price.
