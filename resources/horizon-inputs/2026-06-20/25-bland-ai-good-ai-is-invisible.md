## Why this matters for Horizon — a foundational success-metric insight

This is the **most pedagogically loaded single sentence** I've filed today:

> ***"The best AI does not impress your customer. It just works, and nobody notices."***

This belongs at the top of Horizon's M3 (purpose-based assessment) module. It is the **operational version** of the warning Input 2 makes (Museum of Meaningless Metrics) and the affirmative version of the warning Input 7 makes (3 AI traps — especially Trap #3, believing AI will replace everything via hype).

The frame is so clean it deserves naming as a Horizon-canonical principle: **The Invisibility Test.** A Horizon learner shipping an AI feature asks: *"Will a customer praise this for being clever?"* If yes, the design has failed at production-AI's actual job. If no — if the system simply works and nobody mentions it — the design has succeeded.

## Caveat — the marketing genius vs the product reality

Anastasiia S.'s post is praising Bland's **marketing positioning**. Whether Bland's actual phone-agent product hits the invisibility standard is a separate empirical question. Horizon's write-up should distinguish:

- **The positioning insight is universally true and teachable** (don't praise demo-flash; measure customer-not-noticing)
- **The product claim ("we deliver invisible AI") is testable** — Horizon shouldn't endorse Bland's actual product unless someone validates it works as promised under real-world load
