**Non's framing:** URL alone, no caption. Pattern across today's inputs says Horizon material.

## What this is

A **curated meta-directory** of open-source applications, organized by category, with named SaaS-tool equivalents for each. It is to OSS what Product Hunt is to SaaS — but built around the inverse pitch: *every app in this directory is something you can self-host, AGPL/MIT/Apache licensed, and the SaaS it replaces is in the description.*

## Why this is significant for Horizon — convergence at the meta-layer

Across today's 29 prior inputs, a pattern has been emerging: **2026 OSS infrastructure is composable, agentic-aware, and self-hostable.** Caveman (Input 24), codebase-memory-mcp (Input 19), Supabase (Input 20), OKF (Input 27), BigSet (Input 29) — every one is OSS, self-hostable, agent-friendly. Open Apps Pro is the **directory that names the entire trend.**

Horizon learners need to know this directory exists. Knowing about a 575-app OSS catalog converts a learner from *"what SaaS should I buy?"* to *"what should I self-host?"* — which directly enables:
- The **Skill Preservation** principle (Input 26) — self-hosting forces understanding of the underlying systems
- The **no-localhost shipping principle** (already in memory) — self-hosted apps must be deployed publicly to count
- The **OKF architecture commitment** (Input 27) — many apps in the directory are OKF-friendly or could be wrapped to be
- The **license discipline** (Input 29) — the directory makes license-awareness habitual

## Horizon placements

### 1. A persistent navigation surface — *"Find your stack"*

Horizon's landing page or onboarding should include a *"Looking for an OSS alternative to a specific SaaS? Start here"* link out to openapps.pro. Not as a competitor — as a complementary directory. **Horizon is the curriculum that teaches you to build with these; openapps.pro is the catalog of what to build with.**

The two-step pitch:
1. *Find your tools at openapps.pro*
2. *Learn how to compose them productively at Horizon*

This is a non-competitive positioning that benefits both sides.

### 2. Horizon Research paper #9

Paper seed: ***"The OSS Self-Hosting Renaissance of 2026: A Directory-Level Read of Where Build-Don't-Buy Is Winning."*** Anchored on openapps.pro's 575+ catalog with cross-references to which Horizon-curriculum modules teach which categories. Particularly relevant for Thai SEIC / depa stakeholders evaluating whether to invest in proprietary platforms vs OSS-native ones (same audience as the OKF paper from Input 27).

### 3. Pinterest library cards — alternatives filter

The Pinterest library (Input 12) gains a new filter chip: **"Alternatives to..."** Cards in this filter tag each OSS app with the SaaS it replaces (e.g. *"AppFlowy → Notion alternative"*, *"Plausible → Google Analytics alternative"*, *"Plane → Linear alternative"*). Cards link out to openapps.pro for the app's full profile.

### 4. M4 curriculum addition

M4 (AI in the physical world / real working systems) should include a lesson: ***"Choosing your stack — how to evaluate OSS alternatives without falling for catalog overwhelm."*** Decision criteria: license (per Input 29's AGPL caveat), maintenance signal (commits in last 90 days, issues velocity), community size, deployment complexity, integration with your existing tooling. Use openapps.pro's category pages as worked-example research surfaces.

## License discipline applies here too

openapps.pro is a *directory*, not a license arbiter. The apps in the directory carry their own licenses, ranging from MIT/Apache (permissive) to AGPL-3.0 (copyleft, like BigSet from Input 29). Horizon's curriculum should always teach learners to **check the license of any specific app before adopting it for productization**, not assume the directory's curation implies license-safe-for-all-uses.

The directory's value is *discovery*. License-decision is downstream.
