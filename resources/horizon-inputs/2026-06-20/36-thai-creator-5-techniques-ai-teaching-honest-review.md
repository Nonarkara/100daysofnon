# Input 36 — Thai creator's "5 techniques for AI to teach you" post — honest competitive-landscape review

**Source:** Thai-language post by *"หัวหน้าแบงค์ fullfunnel"* — appears to be a Thai-language AI-for-business influencer / educator. Comment-trigger lead magnet via the keyword *"cowork."* Long hashtag list including `#หัวหน้าแบงค์fullfunnel #Claude #AI #ใช้AI #ธุรกิจAI #aiforbusiness #สอนclaude #anthropic`.

**Non's direct question:** *"is this even true?"* — not a *file-this* directive, but an *assess-this* directive.

## The 5 techniques summarized

1. **Make AI plan all steps first, then walk through one at a time** — prompt: *"plan the whole thing first, show me how many steps, then teach me step by step. Don't skip ahead until I finish and understand this step."*
2. **Complete one step at a time, don't move on** — keep asking for one step only.
3. **YOU do the work, not AI** — *"don't finish it for me; tell me where to click, what to type, step by step, and let me do it myself."* Goal: next week you can do it without asking.
4. **Tell AI you're a complete beginner** — *"I'm a total beginner. Explain like teaching a child. Don't use technical terms I don't understand."* Set in the model's personalization.
5. **If stuck, ask again** — *"I don't understand this part, explain again."* Models don't get annoyed.

## Honest assessment — what's true, what's oversold

### What's broadly true and aligned with Horizon's curriculum

- **Technique 1 (plan first, then execute step-by-step)** is the *Plan-and-Execute* pattern from Input 1's Top 9 Agentic Workflows. Real, well-attested, works.
- **Technique 4 (declare your level)** is genuinely effective — models adapt to declared context. Aligned with Input 16's *"good boss your AI deserves"* principle of briefing.
- **Technique 5 (re-ask without shame)** is true — models don't get annoyed, and re-asking is one of the highest-leverage learner habits. Aligns with the *brief-first / ask-back-first* primitive from Input 10.
- **Technique 3's underlying principle** (don't let AI do everything for you while you're learning) IS the **Skill Preservation principle** from Input 26 (Nature, June 2026 — *"physicians using AI for colonoscopy detection lost the skill themselves"*). The post's tactic is sound; the underlying mechanism is real.

### What's partly true and needs nuance

- **Technique 2 (one step at a time, never move on)** works for *early learning* but **conflicts with modern long-running agents** ([[Input 32 — Anthropic Fable Prompting Guide]] — *"it now runs for hours; check progress instead of watching"*). Beginners benefit from one-at-a-time; intermediate operators benefit from long-running with audit checkpoints. The post presents technique 2 as universal; the right framing is *"use this when you're learning; switch to reasons-and-boundaries when you've learned."*
- **Technique 3's "always have YOU do the work"** is *correct for learning*, *misleading for production*. The goal of using AI isn't always to learn — sometimes it's to ship the deliverable. Skill Preservation (Input 26) is about *deliberate practice days,* not *every interaction is a lesson.* The post conflates the two modes.

### What's marketing-shaped overclaim

- **"จากคนใช้ไม่เป็น กลายเป็นใช้ AI ทำงานแทนได้ เก่งขึ้นใน 1 วัน"** — *"From not knowing how to use AI to using it to do your work — get better in 1 day."* The 1-day claim is content-marketing hyperbole. Real fluency is weeks, not a day. One day can give you the *unlock* (the moment you realize you can prompt for help) but not *fluency.* Horizon's positioning should never make the 1-day claim — it's an EGO-VOID violation (per [[feedback-teach-dont-silently-fix]]).
- **"ปัญหาคือคุณสั่งให้เขาสอนคุณผิดวิธี"** — *"The problem is you're commanding it to teach you the wrong way."* True but oversimplified. There are other genuine failure modes (wrong tool for task, wrong model for use case, wrong expectation of what AI can currently do, hallucination, the Skill Preservation problem itself). The post implies *one root cause*; reality has *multiple*.

### The lead-magnet pattern

- The comment-trigger *"cowork"* is the standard Thai-language educational-creator playbook (same pattern as Inputs 9, 13, 16, 17, 23 from earlier today). Not deceptive; it's the engagement-funnel standard. Horizon should be aware that its target audience encounters this format constantly and may have varying levels of trust in it.

## Net assessment

**Mostly true. Genuinely useful for the absolute-beginner audience. Aligned with the principles Horizon's curriculum has been building today, but presented as tactics-without-principles.**

**The difference between this post and Horizon's positioning:**
- This post teaches the **tactic** (the specific prompt to say)
- Horizon teaches the **principle** (why it works, when to use it, when *not* to)

Both have value. The post is better for someone who needs a 5-minute confidence-unlock today. Horizon is better for someone building durable practice over months. **They are complementary, not competitive.** A Horizon learner who has not yet read this kind of post might benefit from it as an on-ramp. A Horizon learner who only reads posts like this without principles will plateau within weeks at the *can-prompt-but-can't-orchestrate* level.

## Why this matters for Horizon

This input is *competitive-landscape data* more than *new substance*. It confirms three things Horizon's design should know:

1. **The Thai-language audience reads posts like this constantly.** Horizon's tone needs to differentiate from this register — not by being harder or more academic, but by being **principled, honestly-caveat-ed, and continuous-with-real-practice** rather than tactic-of-the-day.

2. **The 5 techniques are genuinely useful** — Horizon should incorporate the underlying principles (Plan-and-Execute, declare-your-level, ask-back, Skill Preservation in deliberate-practice mode) without aping the *"in 1 day"* marketing register.

3. **The "always do the work yourself" tactic conflicts with the long-running-agent reality.** Horizon's M1 should teach learners to distinguish *learning mode* (do it yourself) from *production mode* (let AI do, audit selectively) — and to know which mode they're in. That's a nuance the Thai-language educational community is broadly missing.
