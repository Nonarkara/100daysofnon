## The substance — the infographic's 12 named concepts, in order

### Top half — the system as it stands

1. **PUBLISH-OR-PERISH** — *"Pressure to keep publishing to survive in academia. Publications shape hiring, promotion, funding, and status."*

2. **METRIC PRODUCTIVISM** — *"Belief that academic worth can be measured through: publication counts, journal rankings, citation scores, impact metrics."*

3. **SYMBOLIC CAPITAL** — *"Publications function as symbolic capital. They convey recognition, prestige, and professional advantage."* (Bourdieusian framing.)

4. **ACADEMIC CAPITALISM** — *"Universities operate like competitive markets. Knowledge is treated as a commodity. Journal article becomes a measurable unit of output."* (Slaughter & Leslie 1997, et seq.)

5. **BIOINFORMATIONAL CAPITALISM** — *"Scholarly writing is structured for databases, search engines, citation systems, and machine processing."*

6. **PERFORMATIVITY** — *"Drawing on Lyotard, academic knowledge is judged more by output and efficiency than by truth or intellectual depth."* (Lyotard, *The Postmodern Condition*, 1979 — real, foundational.)

### Bottom half — what AI does to this system

7. **WHAT AI CHANGES** — *"LLMs can now help produce: abstracts, drafts, literature reviews, peer review tools, and citation maps. It joins the system as a labor accelerator."* Visualized as: **WRITING → AUTOMATION; REVIEW → AUTOMATION; PUBLICATION → RECOGNITION**.

8. **WHAT BEGINS TO BREAK** — the system's quality-calibration loop. With more outputs at lower marginal cost, signal-to-noise collapses; peer review cannot scale; recognition uncouples from contribution.

9. **THE SEVERED PRIMARY** — the primary scholarly acts (reading, thinking, conversing, learning) become disconnected from the public-output layer (publication). What was supposed to be the *expression* of scholarship becomes its *substitute*.

10. **EPISTEMIC INJURY** — the system itself begins to lose the capacity to know what it knows. Knowledge production accelerates while knowledge-accumulation decelerates.

11. **WHAT COMES AFTER?** — implied alternatives (the infographic lists several short bullets — too small to fully resolve, but the structure suggests: slow scholarship, open peer review, post-metric assessment, monograph revival, oral-tradition return).

12. **MAIN TAKEAWAY** — *"AI is not undoing scholarship. It is exposing the limits of a system that treated scholarship as production output."*
