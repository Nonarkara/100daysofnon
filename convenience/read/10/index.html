THE CONVENIENCE · INTERLUDE C

The Frame

10 of 19

The dashcam in Wichai's Vios recorded onto a sixty-four-gigabyte card and overwrote itself every nine days. He had bought it in 2021, after a Fortuner clipped his mirror on the tollway and the other driver's insurance man talked him out of a fair number politely, over three weeks, in air-conditioning. Since then he trusted the camera more than he trusted anything else about the car, including the brakes. He never watched the files. Owning them was the point. The card sat in its slot behind the mirror mount, and on Sunday mornings, when he slept until noon with the fan turned on his face, the keys stayed in the bowl by the rice cooker, one unwatched object among the rest, in a house that believed itself unobserved.

Pui took the card out at eight and had it back in the slot by nine.

The reader cost 129 baht from a phone-accessory stall at the market and plugged into her phone, and nine days of the Vios took most of the hour to copy. She did it at the table, facing the bedroom door, the phone lying flat and working, unremarkable, exactly what it appeared to be. The camera took sound as well as road. That was the part he had never thought about, because he had bought it to watch other drivers. Nine days of cab audio: the ramp queue at the port, his calls, the radio, the toll receipts being wedged above the visor. Some Sundays she was in the footage herself, passenger side, temple days and market days, her own voice arriving small and slightly ahead of her memory of it.

The temple footage came through her cousin Od. Wat Lam Duan had carried cameras since the donation box went missing in 2019 — Od had installed them, being the relative who was good with wires, and he serviced them twice a year and exported the files nobody ever asked for. She asked for the memorial weeks. Her father's merit-making came around every January, and she told Od their mother wanted a video made for the aunt in Khon Kaen, all the years together, and Od copied five Januaries onto a 99-baht thumb drive and asked nothing, being family, and being besides the kind of man whom cameras made feel useful.

The rest was the staff videos, New Year 2023 and New Year 2024, six minutes each, HR's phone on its tripod. Altogether, using the same discipline the floor paid her to use on strangers, she held eleven hours and forty minutes of footage in which she herself appeared. Most people would be surprised how much of themselves is on file. Most people never ask for the export.

In the second week of January the tool got a new build. The update ran during the day shift, and when the night women logged in on the ninth the interface had moved two buttons and grown a tab. The tab said PREVIEW, in English, and sat grey. Sombat taped a memo to the pillar by his desk saying not to touch anything new until head office said, and head office, in the manner of head office, never said. The tab stayed grey through every client ticket. On calibration tickets with no client code — the kind the tool allowed and nobody assigned — it woke.

What it did was continuation. You gave it an arc — clips of one subject, flags set, confidence rated — and a horizon, which was a number typed into a field, and it rendered forward: soft grey footage, watermarked DEV in the corner, twenty to forty seconds of probable next frames, stamped with a date anyway, neatly, in the future tense. The engineers presumably used it to check whether the model had understood an arc or only memorized it. If it had understood, the continuation held. Pui read the tab's help text once, completely — her only method — and then used the feature for what it was for.

The shift ended at six and the overtime ended when Sombat's clock said it ended, and after that the floor belonged to whoever stayed, which was her. The songthaew idled at the fence and filled with cardigans and left.

"The queue runs the same speed at six as at two," Daeng said once, at the gate, not boarding yet.

"I like it when it's quiet," Pui said.

Daeng looked at her for the length of a timeline. Then she boarded. On the floor you asked nothing further; that was the courtesy and the whole of it.

At the terminal, seat 14, badge on the desk, Pui cut herself out of the footage and dropped the clips into calibration tickets, and the tickets opened with the line every ticket opened with, English over Thai: MARK THE FRAME WHERE THE PERSON STOPS MEANING WHAT THEY SAY. The subject was her. She did not find that this changed the work. The memorial of 2019: her wai to the abbot, her thanks to the aunts for coming, and nothing to flag anywhere in it, the whole clip meant. The memorial of 2021: flag at 00:41, where the thanks to the aunts went on being made rather than happening. The Vios, the twelfth of January just past: his voice asking something about the envelope, her own voice answering ไม่เป็นไร, and the flag set on the second syllable. Confidence five. She rated her own clips although there was no one anywhere to submit them to, because the rating was part of the pass, and she did not do partial passes.

For the timestamps she bought a grey school notebook from the stationery shop by the Bang Sue market — the horse on the cover, the multiplication tables printed on the back unchanged since she was a girl in Nakhon Pathom — and ruled nothing, added nothing, only wrote: dates down the left, timestamps down the right, her hand small and level. No words. Words could be read by anyone who found them. Numbers without their footage were just numbers, a column meaning nothing to a polite man with a small book, or to Wichai, or to anyone alive but the woman holding the other half of each entry in her head.

It should be said what her flags were worth, because everything that follows rests on it. She had come to the floor in June 2023. Singapore sampled sixty of her flags a month against its senior reviewers, and in eight months the seniors had moved her flag exactly once, by two frames, and moved it back the next day with a note in English she photographed and did not need translated. She was not proud of this in any way she could use. It was simply the case, as her hands were small and her nails were short, and what it meant was that when she flagged her own footage there was no room left in the arithmetic where a doubt could sit down. If she had been a worse worker, there would have been room.

In the third week of January he put her into the edge of the bathroom doorframe, once, with the flat of his forearm, over a sentence about the envelope that had come out of her at the wrong pitch. He was drunk to the degree that makes a man accurate rather than wild, and it was over in a second, and he was asleep in four minutes. The mark on her shoulder took twelve days to go down. She cleared two hundred and forty clips a night that week. On the following Sunday's card there was nothing to flag — he had driven to the port and back in silence, signaling early, careful at the school crossing — and she copied it anyway. Absence was also footage.

She began the horizons small. Thirty days: the render gave her back the rooms it had been fed, the envelope on a tenth rendered as an envelope on a tenth, grey and soft and plausible. Ninety days: the collector's Wave at the soi mouth, the small book, the wai, drawn clean out of the average of its appearances. The machine drew what the labels knew. Nothing in it was news, and that was itself the result: the continuation held. The model had not memorized her arc. It had understood it.

On the twelfth of March she assembled the whole of it — the marriage, sixty-one clips, 2019 to that morning, every flag hers, every rating five — and typed into the horizon field the largest number it would accept.

The progress bar took forty minutes. The day shift came in past the guard's radio, luk thung at dawn, and the floor warmed by the two degrees the bodies brought with them, and nobody looked at seat 14, because seat 14 staying late had stopped being information months ago. The clips came out in order, stamped forward, dwelling where the data was thick and skipping where it thinned: 2025 twice — a room she knew with less furniture in it, a counter at a district office and a form going across it. 2026: the envelope, still on the tenth, the figure serviced and not smaller. 2028: boxes, a truck's tailgate, the render losing confidence at its edges, past the reach of what it had been fed. Then one more clip, twenty-six seconds, stamped with a full date in 2031, printed as neatly as the rest of the guesswork.

It opened on the outside of a house she had never seen. Two storeys, the lower half tiled, a blue water tank on a steel stand, a gate with a chain worked through it, and at the soi mouth a motorcycle-taxi vest with the queue name and the district lettered across the back. Samut Prakan. The district did not surprise her. She had read a drop-off address off his phone in December — once, completely, filed and not reopened — and had put it away in the column of things that were only numbers. Then the render cut inside. A kitchen: gas bottle, dish rack, a calendar from a shop she couldn't name. A woman at the sink with her back one-quarter turned to the camera, older through the shoulders than the arc had any right to know, hands in the water, washing. A man's voice off-frame, low, asking something with numbers in it. The hands kept moving. At the eleventh second the hands went on moving exactly as before, except that they were no longer washing anything.

She set the flag at eleven seconds. The hand did it before the rest of her had voted; that was what the hand was for. The confidence field she left empty — there was no one on any floor of any building to submit this to. She let the clip run out. The voice finished its numbers; the woman at the sink answered; the answer took two seconds, and meant nothing, and was flagged already. The clip ended on the dish rack.

She did not scrub back. She watched it once.

The dev build kept no output — that was the other thing the tab said, in smaller English — and when the ticket closed, the twenty-six seconds went wherever unpaid renders go. From her bag she took the notebook, worked the rubber band off, and wrote the date on the left and 00:11 on the right, under the column of dates and numbers already going down the page, and worked the band back on. Once was what there was. She had never in her life needed a second pass.

There was an objection available, and walking out through the aluminum door into the March heat she gave it the floor. The preview was arithmetic. It had never been to Samut Prakan. It had read a district off a vest because vests were simply in its diet, one input among the rest; it had built the house out of the average of houses, the tank out of the average of tanks; it had put her at a sink because the labels she fed it were full of sinks. A guess, fueled entirely by her own honesty, wearing a date it had no right to — decoration on a probability. All of that was available, and all of it was true, and she let the whole objection stand, unanswered, the way you leave a door open for someone who never comes. It did not move the flag. The flag was hers. In eight months, nobody had moved her flag.

In the weeks after, she began to arrange things. She did it the way she cleared a queue, one ticket at a time, no partial passes. On the inside cover of the notebook she wrote a list, seven lines, the hand small and level. The first line had been done since November; she wrote it anyway, and crossed it out, for the completeness. On a Tuesday she counted the cardigan money on the bathroom floor with the door locked and the water running — thirty-eight thousand baht by the end of March, folded once, in denominations that had never seen a passbook. On one of her days off she went out in her good blouse on an errand she told no one about, and came back before he did, and made dinner.

At the stationery shop by the Bang Sue market, on the last Sunday of the month, she bought a second notebook, the same horse on the cover, the same tables on the back, fourteen baht, and the woman at the till, unasked, pulled a rubber band off a bundle of receipts and gave it to her for nothing. The first notebook was two pages from full.
